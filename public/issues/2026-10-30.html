30 October 2026

Anthropic's first public accounts show revenue up more than tenfold

The S-1 filed this week puts revenue at 16.3 billion dollars for the first half of 2026, against 1.2 billion dollars in the same period a year earlier. The headline net loss of about 80 billion dollars is mostly not cash: Scott Galloway reads roughly 72 billion dollars of it as a one off charge from converting convertible debt into equity, which leaves adjusted operating income near 1.6 billion dollars. Compute alone cost about 9 billion dollars in the half, already more than the 7.3 billion dollars spent across all of 2025.

Source: Pivot, 27 October 2026, Anthropic's IPO Filing, Elon's Talarico Obsession, and Elizabeth Holmes' Box Office Surprise

The compute bill and five customers are the risk Anthropic has to sell past

The same filing discloses more than 600 billion dollars committed to future compute and cloud deals, with outside estimates of the obligation ranging from 400 billion to 800 billion dollars, and five customers accounting for about a quarter of revenue over the first nine months. The risk factors run to 94 pages and include a warning that advanced AI could pose catastrophic or existential risk to humanity. Galloway also argues the comparison with OpenAI is unfair in Anthropic's favour, because Anthropic books the full value of token sales while OpenAI recognises only its own share.

Source: Pivot, 27 October 2026, Anthropic's IPO Filing, Elon's Talarico Obsession, and Elizabeth Holmes' Box Office Surprise

Microsoft and Alphabet reported the same AI boom and got opposite verdicts

Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson walks through why one set of cloud numbers was rewarded and the other punished. Microsoft posted Azure growth of 48 percent, disclosed Azure's dollar revenue for the first time at 33.2 billion dollars, came in under its own capital spending projections and guided calendar 2027 capex to around 170 billion dollars. Alphabet fell about 10 percent on results that included Google Cloud revenue roughly doubling to about 31 billion dollars, with investors focused on free cash flow and a much larger spending year ahead.

Source: Prof G Markets, 29 October 2026, Microsoft Rises, Google Tanks: Why Investors Are Polarized On Big Tech

Microsoft has offered buyouts to about a quarter of its workforce

Ed Elson closes on Microsoft's voluntary buyout program, which was reported on 26 October and covers roughly 60,000 employees. The framing from the company is an efficiency push as AI reshapes how work gets done, not financial distress, which is notable against a quarter that beat its own guidance. It is the clearest signal yet that the AI capital spending story and the headcount story are now the same story.

Source: Prof G Markets, 29 October 2026, Microsoft Rises, Google Tanks: Why Investors Are Polarized On Big Tech

The Justice Department is asking questions about Nvidia's Hugging Face deal

Reuters reported on 28 October that the DOJ is examining whether Nvidia's 12.9 billion dollar acquisition of Hugging Face, agreed in September and expected to close in the first half of 2027, would hand Nvidia a fundamental distribution point for AI and let it disadvantage rivals. It lands on top of a multi year DOJ inquiry into Nvidia's sales practices and equity stakes, a French antitrust probe, and a reported look at whether the 20 billion dollar Groq licensing and hiring arrangement was structured to avoid merger review.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 29 October 2026, The AI Rankings Just Got Reshuffled

The main model leaderboard has been rebuilt to defeat benchmark gaming

Artificial Analysis has shipped version 5 of its Intelligence Index, which now takes 60 percent of its score from private test sets specifically to stop models being tuned to public benchmarks. The reshuffle is brutal for open weights: Kimi K3.1 Pro fell from fourth at launch to ninth, with its score dropping from 55 to 46, and Grok 4.7 lost 12 points. The gap between the best open model and the top of the table widened from 3 points to 12.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 29 October 2026, The AI Rankings Just Got Reshuffled

Google has shipped a frontier model aimed first at cyber defenders

Gemini 4 Carbon arrives about two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, with Google claiming state of the art results on cybersecurity and long horizon tasks, including 88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro and 71.0 percent on CWE-bench. Artificial Analysis puts it first on the rebuilt Intelligence Index at 59, just ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58. Access starts with trusted defenders through Google's Fairwind Program, and the trade offs are real: it sits behind Claude on the Coding Agent Index and its hallucination rate rose from 4 percent to 11 percent.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 29 October 2026, The AI Rankings Just Got Reshuffled

DeepMind's Genie 4 lets you rewrite a running simulation by talking to it

Announced on 27 October, Genie 4 is pitched as the first real time world model with native audio, where a user can edit a simulation while it is running using plain language. Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as a guiding agent inside the loop, and outputs carry SynthID watermarks. It goes to trusted testers as a research preview first, then to AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie, with robotics and film production named as the target uses.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 28 October 2026, AI Can Now Create Worlds You Can Change in Real Time

Anthropic has bought a one person biotech company

Anthropic announced on 27 October that it has acquired Mithril Medicine, a single founder biotech working on preclinical muscular dystrophy therapies, with founder Cody Tranbarger joining the company and terms undisclosed. It follows the roughly 400 million dollar Coefficient Bio deal in April. The pattern is a frontier lab buying scientific work rather than scientific talent alone, days before filing to go public.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 28 October 2026, AI Can Now Create Worlds You Can Change in Real Time

A jury in Los Angeles is now hearing the social media addiction case

Jury selection began on 28 October before Judge Carolyn Kuhl in a bellwether trial alleging that Meta and Snap designed their products to addict minors, through features such as endless scroll and Snapstreaks. TikTok and YouTube settled before trial and Meta settled with one of the two teenage plaintiffs. Opening statements are expected in early November with the trial running to mid December, after an earlier 2026 case in which a jury found Meta and YouTube liable and awarded 6 million dollars.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, 29 October 2026, Rohit Chopra's AI nightmare; Meta faces new trial on social media addiction in LA

A former federal consumer regulator says Washington is not ready for an AI unwind

Rohit Chopra, who ran the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau until he was removed under the current administration and is now California's consumer protection secretary, argues that the federal government is badly prepared for the fallout if the AI trade turns. His concern is less the valuations than the plumbing underneath them: who is carrying the credit, the compute commitments and the consumer exposure when the write downs start. He has said he intends to look at how AI and chatbots are being deployed across sectors.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, 29 October 2026, Rohit Chopra's AI nightmare; Meta faces new trial on social media addiction in LA

The Energy Department's AI program is being framed as a second Manhattan Project

Barnett Koven of Accenture Federal Services describes work on the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, an AI for science program that its backers compare directly to the Manhattan Project, drawing in the NNSA, Sandia, DARPA and IARPA. He says 26 companies originally signed the Genesis commitment, including the frontier model labs, and that the build underway is a backbone for scientific research covering data capture and access control. Worth noting that the episode is sponsored by Accenture Federal Services and the guest is an executive there.

Source: ChinaTalk, 29 October 2026, The Manhattan Project, Take Two

Export control enforcement is being rebuilt around entity resolution

The same conversation makes the case that the binding constraint on export controls is enforcement capacity rather than rule writing, and that AI is now doing the work of mapping procurement networks. Koven gives a worked example of a shell company chain traced through seven corporate entities to a People's Liberation Army procurement arm, for the Bureau of Industry and Security and Treasury. He also describes a multi year effort to win roughly 100 million dollars more in funding for BIS, which has long been the smallest agency with the largest mandate.

Source: ChinaTalk, 29 October 2026, The Manhattan Project, Take Two

RAND says the United States is running an acceleration strategy by default

Three RAND researchers unpack a new Perspective that catalogues seven archetypal strategies for superintelligence, from dominance and co-development through to moratorium and deterrence, each with its own win state and its own failure mode, plus five pivot factors that should decide which one a country picks. Their headline finding is that the US has not chosen: it is accelerating by default. RAND is also costing a security architecture catalogue of 106 capabilities, on the principle that a strategy without a price tag is just a vision, and the guests forecast a rough patch in cyber over the next 12 to 24 months as frontier offensive capability reaches open weight models.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 28 October 2026, Freedom of Action: America's ASI Strategy, with RAND's Joel Predd, Ben Boudreaux and Matt Chessen

a16z's new list argues production capacity is now the deterrent

The firm's AD50 for 2026 sorts 50 companies into systems, factories and inputs, with the argument that deterrence now rests on the ability to manufacture rather than the ability to design. The numbers cited are large: an Army enterprise contract with a 20 billion dollar ceiling over ten years for Anduril, a 392 million dollar Navy production contract and a planned 3 billion dollar shipyard for Saronic, and a 250 million dollar raise in August for Neros, which is targeting a million drones a year by 2028. Read it with the firm's position in mind: a16z is an investor in many of the companies named, the contract figures are ceilings rather than obligated spend, and several of the manufacturing numbers are company stated targets.

Source: the a16z Podcast, 29 October 2026, The Startups Rebuilding American Industry, The AD50
