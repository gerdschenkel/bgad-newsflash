An outside audit says Anthropic is now building models about twice as fast as a year ago

The AI Daily Brief walked through a new report from METR, the third party evaluation group, which reviewed Anthropic's internal data between 12 August and 29 October. METR found the company shipping capability gains at roughly twice the pace of a year earlier, with about two years of benchmark progress compressed into the last twelve months. It attributes most of that to AI tools used inside the development process rather than to extra training compute, and reports that staff using the most capable internal model were around twelve times more efficient. Three models have finished training and have not been released, and Anthropic held redaction rights over the roughly one hundred page public version.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 6 November 2026, Companies Are Spending 5x More on AI Now

Enterprise AI spending is running at 176 billion dollars, more than five times last year

The episode's main segment is the new Menlo Ventures enterprise AI report. It puts deployment spending at about 176.8 billion dollars by September 2026, up roughly 5.4 times from 33 billion in 2025, with agents accounting for much of the jump and a lot of buyers running over budget. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google now take about 69 per cent of that deployment spend between them, up from half. On enterprise API spend specifically, Anthropic moved from 40 to 55 per cent, OpenAI from 27 to 31, and Google fell from 21 to 9.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 6 November 2026, Companies Are Spending 5x More on AI Now

Agents, not people, are now generating about four fifths of the tokens

The same report finds agentic traffic has taken over consumption. On OpenRouter, agents account for roughly 80 per cent of tokens, about 130.9 trillion a week, which is around seven times human driven usage. That is the mechanism behind the pricing shift the show describes, from per seat licences towards per token billing. For anyone buying AI this year it changes the budgeting question from how many staff will use it to how much work the agents will do.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 6 November 2026, Companies Are Spending 5x More on AI Now

About four trillion dollars has come off the AI trade, and the data centre pipeline is freezing

All In's latest episode leads on the market damage following the midterms, which it sizes at roughly four trillion dollars of lost value concentrated in the AI complex, and on data centre construction being slowed or halted. Read the framing with care. All four hosts run venture or investment firms with direct exposure to AI infrastructure, so an argument that the sell off is an overreaction and the buildout will resume is also an argument for their own positions.

Source: All In, 7 November 2026, Midterm Blood Bath, 4T AI Sell Off, Data Center Freeze, What's Next for Trump

OpenAI says one of its models has refuted a forty year old complexity conjecture

The science segment covers OpenAI's claim that one of its models produced a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a longstanding conjecture in computational complexity about how hard certain problems have to be. If it holds, it is the first time a model has overturned a result of that standing rather than reproducing known mathematics. Outside experts were still working through the proof when the episode was recorded, so treat it as claimed and not yet settled.

Source: All In, 7 November 2026, Midterm Blood Bath, 4T AI Sell Off, Data Center Freeze, What's Next for Trump

Anthropic is at about an eighty billion dollar run rate, and the trillion and a half valuation is wobbling

Latent Space ran a weekend deep dive on the Anthropic S-1 with Jamin Ball of Altimeter. The numbers discussed: a year end target of a 100 billion dollar revenue run rate against roughly 80 billion now, investor scepticism about the 1.5 trillion dollar listing valuation, and some estimates cut back towards 1 trillion. Polymarket's odds on the IPO landing by its expected date fell 18 points. With the Nasdaq off about 10 per cent, the listing has become the sector's price discovery event.

Source: Latent Space, 7 November 2026, Deep Dive, Anthropic S-1 Myths and Mythos with Jamin Ball, Altimeter

Prof G Markets says the AI boom has run out of easy money

Ed Elson's short read argues the AI trade has lost its momentum and that the gains which carried it through 2026 are now much harder to find. The piece is a position rather than a data release, and the show does not put numbers behind it. It is worth noting mainly because it lands in the same week as the sell off and the Anthropic listing, and because Prof G has been one of the more consistently sceptical mainstream voices on AI valuations.

Source: Prof G Markets, 7 November 2026, The AI Boom Is Running Out Of Easy Money

Figma's CFO on what actually got automated in finance, and what went wrong

Praveer Melwani describes moving the finance function from reporting on last month to shaping next quarter, with collections and expense analysis the two workflows genuinely running on AI, plus AI assisted diligence in acquisitions. His warning is the one finance teams are hitting now: models produce plausible but poor work, so the guardrails matter more than the pilot. His practical advice is to migrate successful one off experiments onto a central systems layer rather than leaving them scattered. The hosts are a16z partners who invest in exactly this category.

Source: The a16z Show, 5 November 2026, The New CFO, How AI Is Rewriting the Finance Function with Figma CFO Praveer Melwani

The AI safety coalition had a very good midterm

Decoded by POLITICO reports that a broad coalition of AI safety aligned candidates and campaigns won decisively on 3 November, and looks at what the group does with that leverage now. It is a notable reversal: industry super PAC money was deployed against several of these candidates, including against legislators who had authored state level frontier AI bills. The practical question for the next session is whether safety legislation now moves at federal level or stays with the states.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, formerly POLITICO Tech, 6 November 2026, The blue wave hits Anthropic's IPO

One person has set American AI policy for two years, and that is about to stop

The show's argument is that Trump has had effectively unilateral command of AI policy since taking office, and that the midterm result almost certainly ends it. A Democratic Congress gets a say on data centre siting, on power and water allocation, and on the federal preemption question that the industry has been pushing. The episode also works through which technology companies came out of the election better and worse placed.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, 5 November 2026, Trump's AI isolation

In a biology benchmark, a constant guess beat the sophisticated models

Fabian Theis of Helmholtz Munich told Latent Space that in the 2025 Arc Virtual Cell Challenge, constant baselines, not even linear ones, often beat purpose built perturbation prediction models. His read is not that the models are useless but that the evaluation metrics are badly calibrated and reward an uninformative average, because most genes barely move under perturbation. With better metrics, deep learning does beat the mean. It is a useful caution for anyone reading AI for science benchmark claims.

Source: Latent Space, 6 November 2026, Controlling Biology with Fabian Theis, Helmholtz Munich

The lab skills barrier is thinning, and robotics is the last physical brake

Joshua Monrad of Sentinel Bio argued on The Cognitive Revolution that recent in lab uplift trials show frontier models materially lowering the hands on know how that has long constrained wet lab work, with multimodal troubleshooting a large part of it. His conclusion is that once laboratory automation closes the remaining physical gap, the practical brake on misuse largely goes with it. That is the case he makes for putting controls at the DNA synthesis layer and pooling signals across providers rather than relying on any single order tripping an alarm.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 5 November 2026, KYC Becomes KYA, Sentinel Bio's Joshua Monrad on DNA Screening, Uplift Studies and Agent Monitoring

A French bank lost around 118 million euros to a cyberattack, and European banking noticed

Credit Mutuel Arkea has confirmed a cyberattack that allowed fraudulent transactions, with reporting putting the loss at roughly 118 million euros taken from the bank's own equity capital rather than from customer accounts. That detail is what has rattled the sector: the attack hit the institution's balance sheet directly. European banks had broadly assumed this class of attack was contained by existing controls. Flipped into Digital News over the weekend.

Source: Le Monde in English, 6 November 2026, European banks wake up to scale of vulnerability after cyberattack on French bank, via Digital News on Flipboard
