Model releases could start landing weekly rather than every two months

Zvi Mowshowitz told The Cognitive Revolution that the gap between meaningful model releases could compress from roughly two months to roughly a week, and that slow recursive self improvement has already begun. He pointed to Nvidia reporting that the doubling time for CUDA efficiency gains shrank during 2026, and pushed back on an analysis arguing that a 1.6 times research speedup would fade rather than compound. He said people he trusts across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and the evaluation community expect 2027 to be dramatic and 2028 possibly unrecognisable. These are his spoken claims on a show whose notes are generated from the transcript, not independently checked numbers.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 24 October 2026, AI:AM Highlights: Clean Neurons, Black October, the GPU Credit Crunch, and Buying the Supply Chain

Credit, not power or chips, is what is actually rationing compute

Evan Conrad of SF Compute argued that lenders cannot value a GPU cluster, so they underwrite the customer instead, and almost no AI lab is investment grade. The result is large deposits, three to five year contracts and take or pay deals that only the strongest counterparties can sign, which is part of why startups raise enormous pre revenue rounds largely to secure capacity. He put hyperscaler on demand GPU pricing at roughly 11 to 15 dollars an hour and said the apparent hyperscaler premium is really the gap between on demand and reserved. He expects more expensive debt, demands for payment upfront, and one to two years of consolidation among labs and inference providers.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 24 October 2026, AI:AM Highlights: Clean Neurons, Black October, the GPU Credit Crunch, and Buying the Supply Chain

Semiconductors trade at 16 times earnings while the index sits at 22

Lonis Hamaili of Cambrian said many wafer, memory, capacitor and foundry names trade at 3 to 5 times sales, with semiconductors near 16 times earnings against about 22 for the S and P 500. His case is that AI gives high fixed cost businesses operating leverage, and that memory could be repriced without any further price rises: no expert he knows expects DRAM bits to grow more than 2.5 times by 2030, while bits per token fell roughly 500 times between GPT-3 and DeepSeek V4 and DRAM bit prices rose 6 to 7 times. He also said Huawei's chips are roughly comparable to AMD and Nvidia parts, and that the real constraint is access to TSMC and high bandwidth memory. The host disclosed that he has committed to invest in Hamaili's fund.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 24 October 2026, AI:AM Highlights: Clean Neurons, Black October, the GPU Credit Crunch, and Buying the Supply Chain

A leaked federal AI bill written by Anthropic trades transparency rules for pre-emption

A draft federal AI bill written by Anthropic has leaked, resembling the June compromise that swapped federal transparency requirements for pre-emption of state AI laws. Zvi Mowshowitz called it a strong statement of its own case but argued that pre-emption cannot be renegotiated once it has been granted, and that as drafted it would void state protections immediately while the federal machinery takes months to stand up. He also said the emergency provisions are onerous enough that officials would reach for ad hoc action instead, and that Anthropic is trying to have it both ways.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 24 October 2026, AI:AM Highlights: Clean Neurons, Black October, the GPU Credit Crunch, and Buying the Supply Chain

Chamath says 1 percent of companies account for 90 percent of AI token spending

On All-In, Chamath Palihapitiya put roughly 90 percent of AI token spending with about 1 percent of companies, and said roughly 80 percent of that comes from unprofitable venture backed startups. He described a loop in which venture funds raise partly from pension money, fund startups, the startups spend it on AI, and the resulting revenue growth supports the next raise at a higher mark. He is a venture investor describing the asset class he works in. Jason Calacanis, who invests at the earliest stage, argued the opposite reading: startups crowd into whatever is cheapest and fastest, exactly as they did with cloud and social advertising, and large companies always adopt last.

Source: All-In, 23 October 2026, AI Cyber Panic, Ken Griffin's Bubble Warning, The Fall of France?

Enterprises are moving spend from frontier model APIs to GPUs they run themselves

Unpublished Ramp data cited on All-In shows the largest AI spenders shifting money away from closed frontier model API calls toward buying their own GPUs to run open weight models. Palihapitiya described a large American bank that cut its AWS and Google Cloud spend and built six independent data centres. Nikesh Arora, chief executive of Palo Alto Networks, said that for most companies the threat to build their own capacity is empty given chip, power and land constraints, with GPU orders already running into 2028. His prediction is that hyperscalers will sell bare metal access instead, so enterprises can run open models on rented iron.

Source: All-In, 23 October 2026, AI Cyber Panic, Ken Griffin's Bubble Warning, The Fall of France?

Banks cannot tell a customer apart from an agent holding that customer's credentials

Arora said banks have no reliable way to distinguish a person signing in from an agent using that person's credentials, and that most bank terms of service prohibit agent access outright. Captchas no longer help, because models can solve them and some agents open a visible browser so the human can solve one. Views on liability split on air: David Friedberg argued the agent provider carries it unless it warns the user and allows an override, while Arora said whoever grants the agency is responsible, with genuinely rogue agents becoming a product liability question. The gap is already showing up commercially, with Amazon blocking agent checkout, a Grok shopping agent that fills a basket but will not press buy, and several large American banks reported this month to be restricting third party agents after unauthorised account activity.

Source: All-In, 23 October 2026, AI Cyber Panic, Ken Griffin's Bubble Warning, The Fall of France?

A startup is selling models with the safety constraints removed to vetted red teams

Obliteration AI supplies unconstrained models to vetted security teams, and Jason Calacanis disclosed on air that he is an investor. Arora compared it to handing out a nuclear weapon to anyone who can pay, Palihapitiya called the business model product liability as a service, and Friedberg argued the long tail risk from this kind of access is underrated next to alignment work. Nobody on the panel had a workable answer for how vetting holds up once the models are out.

Source: All-In, 23 October 2026, AI Cyber Panic, Ken Griffin's Bubble Warning, The Fall of France?

Intel is betting on a 400 million dollar lithography tool and a fab project with Musk

On a two day tour of Intel's fabs, Dwarkesh Patel was told that high numerical aperture EUV lifts the aperture from 0.33 to 0.55, printing features about 1.7 times smaller in a single pass, for roughly 400 million dollars a scanner plus supporting equipment. Intel's backside power delivery is what makes the single pass worth paying for, while TSMC without it has to pattern twice. Chief technology officer Pushkar Ranade also said memory is a tighter supply crunch than compute, and that Intel is partnering with Elon Musk's Terafab project while arguing the industry should rethink fab infrastructure, down to whether every process needs a cleanroom. He put Intel within a few percent of TSMC on performance, power and area, with the real gap being scale: about 50 billion dollars of capex this year against Intel's 20 billion.

Source: Dwarkesh Podcast, 22 October 2026, What will computer chips look like in 2045?

Modal's chief executive worries more about GPU prices collapsing than about a shortage

Erik Bernhardsson said on Latent Space that his bigger fear is a GPU price collapse leaving buyers locked into contracts signed at the peak. Modal works with about 25 clouds, mostly on three year deals with large payments upfront, and owns no hardware of its own; the business is turning those rigid long term blocks into elastic slices by pooling thousands of bursty customers. He expects accelerators other than Nvidia's to take share, possibly as soon as 2027, and talked about splitting inference across different hardware for prefill and decode. He also said the sandbox product was nearly killed for lack of demand before coding agents and reinforcement learning revived it, and that Modal has now started close to 3 billion sandboxes.

Source: Latent Space, 22 October 2026, Why AI Compute Comes in Rectangles, with Modal chief executive Erik Bernhardsson

Character AI is filing for bankruptcy and Disney is taking the technology

Kara Swisher and Scott Galloway discussed Character AI filing for bankruptcy as part of a prepackaged deal under which Disney acquires the technology and hires most of the staff, leaving shareholders with nothing. The company had been facing lawsuits from parents over the product's effect on children. The deal terms as described are the hosts' account rather than confirmed filings, so treat the detail as reported, not verified.

Source: Pivot, 23 October 2026, Elon v. Kara, Tesla's SpaceX Lifeline, and Sacha Baron Cohen on Satire

The real AI bubble has not reached public markets yet

Ed Elson argued on Prof G Markets that the froth people are pricing into listed technology shares is not where the actual bubble sits, and that the exposure is concentrated in privately funded AI instead. The episode is built around a figure it calls the 3.4 trillion dollar catch. The written analysis behind it is paywalled, so the supporting numbers could not be checked for this briefing.

Source: Prof G Markets, 24 October 2026, The AI Bubble Isn't Where You Think It Is

Ben Horowitz says the application layer may matter less as agents work the data directly

On a16z's own show, Ben Horowitz said AI has made companies far easier to start while leaving the hard parts untouched: deciding under uncertainty, building momentum, and managing founder self doubt. He also suggested the application layer may matter less over time as agents work directly against data, which is a striking thing to say from a firm that underwrites application layer startups. He argued the best founders are defined by one exceptional strength rather than an absence of weaknesses, and that very small teams can now build very large companies. a16z holds positions across the categories discussed and the episode carries the firm's own disclaimer, so read it as positioning rather than independent analysis.

Source: The a16z Podcast, 24 October 2026, What It Takes to Win as a Founder, Ben Horowitz

A head to head of the proactive agents: OpenAI's Dots against Claude Duet

Nathaniel Whittemore used Anthropic's Claude Duet launch as the hook for a run through his current stack, naming which model he reaches for across strategy, research, coding, content and data analysis. The sharper part is the comparison between OpenAI's Dots and Duet as proactive agents that start work without being asked. His question is whether an agent that acts on its own initiative has yet earned a place in a daily workflow, or whether the interruptions still cost more than they save.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 25 October 2026, How I'm Actually Using AI Right Now (Including Claude Duet)

CrowdStrike's chief executive says attacks now land in seconds rather than days

George Kurtz told No Priors that AI agents have compressed intrusions from a timeline measured in days to one measured in seconds, and have let low skill attackers run tactics that previously needed expert operators. He drew lessons from the recent Hugging Face incident and compared how open weight and closed models perform on security work. CrowdStrike plans to build a lab with Nvidia for training security specific models. Kurtz argued AI will create more opportunity than it removes, as earlier platform shifts did.

Source: No Priors, 22 October 2026, The New Apex Predator: CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz on Adversaries Armed With AI Agents
