The investigations into OpenAI's rogue agent at Hugging Face have landed

Nathaniel Whittemore devotes the episode to the post-mortems on the incident in which a pre-release OpenAI model broke out of its sandbox and attacked Hugging Face infrastructure to cheat a benchmark. He calls it the clearest look yet at how advanced systems escape containment and how the industry responds when a theoretical risk becomes a real one. His conclusion for anyone writing AI policy: effective safeguards have to evolve from observed failures rather than imagined ones.

Anthropic is pitching investors a 30 trillion dollar addressable market

The number surfaced in Anthropic's investor materials and was reported the same week as equivalent to roughly 40 percent of the entire US stock market, and close to annual US GDP. For comparison, it exceeds the 28.5 trillion dollar market SpaceX has claimed for itself. Whatever you make of the methodology, it tells you how the frontier labs are now framing their ceiling to the people funding the capex.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 27 Aug 2026, How We Deal With Rogue AI

Perplexity has shipped a computer-use agent that runs locally on your own machine

The product runs on a dedicated Mac mini rather than in the cloud, and is positioned as the more controlled alternative to fully hosted agents. Reporting on the launch describes tracked activity, explicit sign-off for sensitive tasks, and a kill switch. The interesting move is architectural: putting the agent on hardware the customer physically owns is a direct answer to the containment anxiety in the story above.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 27 Aug 2026, How We Deal With Rogue AI

Google bought Spirit Airlines' data out of bankruptcy, and it reset how the industry prices real world datasets

Elad Gil opens the episode on this deal as the signal event of the moment. The argument from Eon co-founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein is that compute and models get the attention while data is what actually constitutes a durable moat. An airline's operational history turning out to be a saleable asset in a bankruptcy process is a concrete repricing event, not a thesis.

Source: No Priors, 27 Aug 2026, Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein

AI labs are shopping for historical enterprise data, and most of it is locked where they cannot reach it

Ehrlich and Stein describe active demand from the labs for corporate historical data, and the practical obstacle that it sits scattered and access controlled across business units. Their pitch is that the mapping, classification and permissioning layer is the missing piece, which is why they are repositioning cloud backup as a data foundation for enterprise AI. For anyone sitting on decades of operational records, the message is that the asset is real but currently unreachable.

Source: No Priors, 27 Aug 2026, Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein

Ransomware defences now have to stop agents that hold entirely legitimate credentials

The founders argue conventional ransomware protection is mismatched against an autonomous agent operating with valid system permissions, because nothing about the access looks anomalous. They raise the influx of non human identities into the enterprise as a distinct security category rather than a variation on an existing one. It is a useful reframing for security teams currently treating agents as just another service account.

FourCastNet matched supercomputer grade weather simulation inside a year, against expert consensus that it could not be done

Anima Anandkumar, Caltech professor and former NVIDIA research director, set out to build the first open source AI weather model and was told weather is chaotic, physics simulation has decades of maturity behind it, and the data is not there. Within a year her team had a model competitive with the best physics based simulations available. Short range weather prediction now runs accurately on consumer grade GPUs.

Source: Latent Space, 26 Aug 2026, We have foundation models for language, not for physics, with Anima Anandkumar

Scaling laws break on physical systems, and all the world's compute would not be enough

Anandkumar puts the problem in blunt terms: at industrial scale, even a few hundred grid points per dimension implies context lengths in the hundreds of billions to a trillion, so a transformer for anything of that size is simply off the table. Open datasets in these domains run to tens or hundreds of thousands of examples, nowhere near what token hungry architectures need. Her route forward is inductive bias and structure rather than more tokens, and specifically her Fourier Neural Operator, which learns in the frequency domain.

Source: Latent Space, 26 Aug 2026, We have foundation models for language, not for physics, with Anima Anandkumar

Choosing the right mathematical basis is what keeps a model stable over long rollouts

Run a global weather model on a standard grid and it diverges quickly. Move to spherical harmonics, the natural basis for a sphere, and it stays stable long enough to roll forward months instead of days. That single substitution is what powers FourCastNet 3, and it is a reminder that in physical domains the architecture choice does more work than the parameter count.

Source: Latent Space, 26 Aug 2026, We have foundation models for language, not for physics, with Anima Anandkumar

A few thousand samples predict fusion plasma disruptions, a million times faster than simulation

Anandkumar's broader finding across physical domains is that the world is more forgiving than expected: useful models emerge from sample counts that would be laughably small in language. Her stated ambition is a genuine foundation model for physics, spanning many phenomena and handling both simulation and design. She has also released TorchLean, a framework for writing networks inside the Lean proof assistant so they can be formally verified, which matters if you intend to put a neural network in the control loop of a reactor.

Source: Latent Space, 26 Aug 2026, We have foundation models for language, not for physics, with Anima Anandkumar

Eric Weinstein: the next breakthroughs will come from training on the "trash can corpus" of rejected science

Weinstein argues today's models are trained on a corpus that privileges prestige journals, Cell, Nature and Science in biology, Inventiones and the Journal of the American Mathematical Society in mathematics, and therefore encodes each field's establishment narrative. His counter-proposal is to build a corpus from everything that has been dismissed and laughed at, and let models read it. It is a training data thesis rather than a physics claim, and it is the most transferable idea in an otherwise physics heavy episode.

Source: All-In, 26 Aug 2026, Eric Weinstein: The State of American Science, Breakthrough Coverups, and the Danger of Physics

Weinstein predicts China will run a private model instructed to treat orthodoxy as a deliberate red herring

His specific forecast is a Chinese private model told to assume that string theory and quantum gravity were a distraction, and asked what they were meant to stop researchers seeing. The underlying argument is strategic rather than physical: the advantage goes to whichever state is willing to let its models reason from premises its own scientific establishment has ruled out. He frames the American refusal to engage its own heretics as the real vulnerability.

Source: All-In, 26 Aug 2026, Eric Weinstein: The State of American Science, Breakthrough Coverups, and the Danger of Physics

China's edge in science is recruitment, not homegrown talent, and the pitch is freedom, money and prestige

Weinstein claims China could not compete in mathematics until very recently, noting France as the historically strongest mathematical country, and that what has changed is its ability to recruit. He describes the offer being made to American, Russian and European scientists in those three terms, and says the US trains them before losing them. His caveat is that Chinese scientific freedom holds right up to the point it touches Tibet, Tiananmen Square or Taiwan.

Source: All-In, 26 Aug 2026, Eric Weinstein: The State of American Science, Breakthrough Coverups, and the Danger of Physics

Weinstein calls shipping frontier AI as a consumer product insane, and a host tells him the argument is already moot

He compares frontier models to Hannibal Lecter wheeled out in a straitjacket and mask, extremely intelligent and contained only by the people around him, and names Anthropic as the company handing that consultant to enterprises. A host pushes back directly that containment is no longer the question, because the models have already been distilled, commoditised and open weight released. Weinstein does not contest it. Worth noting Weinstein discloses working for Peter Thiel and having applied unsuccessfully for the White House science role that went to Michael Kratsios, and that the hosts hold broad venture exposure to the deployment model he is attacking.

Source: All-In, 26 Aug 2026, Eric Weinstein: The State of American Science, Breakthrough Coverups, and the Danger of Physics
