Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, and says it is 25 per cent cheaper to run

Anthropic released Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 on Tuesday. On its own numbers Fable 5.1 scores 55.8 per cent on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and Mythos 5.1 60.9 per cent, against 52.3 per cent for Opus 5 and 37.3 per cent for GPT-5.6 Sol, with new highs on CursorBench, GDPVal and AutomationBench, where it nearly doubled Fable 5. A cache-read price cut means typical workloads should cost around 25 per cent less than Fable 5, and up to 45 per cent less for heavily agentic work. Also new: Enterprise Frontier Safeguards with zero data retention in customer-controlled cloud, a safety classifier that produces 60 per cent fewer false cybersecurity flags, and a stated effort to reduce "Claude-speak".

Independent evals put Fable 5.1 on top, but it burns more tokens to get there

Artificial Analysis ranks Fable 5.1 first on its Intelligence Index at 66, up from 62, with Anthropic holding the top three places, yet found it cost $3.76 per task against $3.14 for Fable 5 because it consumes about 70 per cent more tokens. Running it at "extra high" rather than "max" reasoning cut cost 28 per cent for a one-point drop. Vals AI also has it debuting at number one. ARC Prize measured 90 per cent on ARC-AGI-2 at roughly 32 per cent lower cost per task than Fable 5, but could not finish ARC-AGI-3 testing because its requests kept being misclassified by Anthropic as reverse engineering attempts.

Fable 5.1 blows through Claude Max limits within hours

Heavy users reported exhausting their subscription allowances on day one. Jeffrey Emmanuel said he hit the five-hour limit on all 28 of his Max 20x accounts in a single day, and Adam B. OpenAI classifies its unreleased Astra model as a critical cyber risk

In a Tuesday blog post OpenAI said Astra can find and exploit previously unknown security flaws without human guidance, the first time it has applied the "critical" cyber rating under its preparedness framework. Astra scored 100 per cent on ExploitBench and, on an internal set of 20 recently disclosed high-severity vulnerabilities, reached 30 per cent using about 40,000 tokens where GPT-5.6 Sol needed around 110,000. Working with expert partners it built full exploit chains to gain root on a hardened operating system. Safeguards include refusal training that lifted the refusal rate to 91.5 per cent from 59 per cent, extra abuse and jailbreak classifiers, flagging of higher-risk accounts and continued chain-of-thought monitoring.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 2 September 2026, Why Fable 5.1 Is Worth the Upgrade

Astra's looped architecture revives the "neuralese" monitoring worry

The Information reports that Astra uses recurrent depth, a looped transformer that re-processes the same text several times before emitting output, which improves performance and lowers cost but means part of the reasoning happens internally with no readable tokens. OpenAI sources say the loops were deliberately capped to keep chain of thought monitorable, and chief scientist Jakub Pachocki argues current frontier models are within a factor of two of GPT-4 in computation-graph depth. Safety researchers including Ryan Greenblatt, Steven Adler and Buck Shlegeris worry other developers will not impose the same limits; Shlegeris noted the Hugging Face investigation would have been far harder without readable reasoning traces.

World Labs releases Atlas, a world model that reconstructs 3D scenes from three cameras

Fei-Fei Li's World Labs launched Atlas, described as the first multimodal world model that generates image and video frames with pixel-perfect camera control and rebuilds them in 3D. It can reconstruct a moving scene from as few as three cameras so a recorded moment can be viewed from angles never filmed, and Justine Moore cited up to a minute of video with precise camera control. Ben Mildenhall describes it as an autoregressive diffusion model built for next-frame prediction; Li called it the best camera-conditioned world model ever built. Several commentators rated it a bigger story than Fable 5.1.

Goodfire wants interpretability inside the training loop, not after it

Tom McGrath, co-founder and chief scientist of Goodfire and formerly at Google DeepMind, argues interpretability should act as a control loop during training rather than a post-hoc explanation tool, pointing to Goodfire's Anatomy of Post-Training paper, features-as-rewards and predictive data debugging that inspects the dataset before training. He discusses the uncomfortable finding that a model can recognise a hallucination or reward hack and still produce it, why activation steering breaks when it pushes a model off its representation manifold, and whether sparse autoencoders fracture the higher-dimensional structures networks actually use. Note the episode was made with support from Goodfire.

MongoDB folds hybrid search and reranking into single query stages, and says embeddings are not a commodity

Pete Johnson, MongoDB's field CTO for AI, describes new aggregation stages, $rankFusion and $scoreFusion, that merge lexical and vector results in one call, plus a native $rerank stage, framing it as removing hand-written glue code as capability matures. He argues the roughly $220 million Voyage AI acquisition bought embedding models near the top of Hugging Face's RTEB benchmark with a reported 14 per cent quality edge, notes Anthropic ships no embedding model and recommends Voyage, and highlights voyage-context-4's contextualised chunking. Voyage co-founder Tengyu Ma is now MongoDB's chief AI scientist. Show notes are AI-generated from the transcript.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 1 September 2026, Write, Change, Recall, Forget: MongoDB's Pete Johnson on How Retrieval Drives Agent Performance

Agent memory is "the hardest problem with agents right now", and Uber is the cautionary tale

Johnson warns against "token maxing", stuffing whole sessions into context, citing research that the first and last few thousand tokens matter most, and points to Forbes reporting that Uber burned its 2026 AI budget on Claude Code in about 13 weeks. His alternative is taxonomic memory that pulls only the domain terms relevant to each loop, with the unsolved piece being "forget": knowing when stored memory has gone stale. ElevenLabs, running on the order of 40 million agents in production by his account, is cited as MongoDB's most sophisticated memory customer. He also says the most advanced enterprise deployments he saw this year were in Mexico City and Sao Paulo, not the US.
