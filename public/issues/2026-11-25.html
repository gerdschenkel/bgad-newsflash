25 November 2026

Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang eleven days after he resigned

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to return Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. The company's filing says the board took input from senior levels of the United States government and concluded the return poses no national security risk. Colette Kress steps back from the interim chief executive role to CFO, and the board has created a National Security and Export Control Committee made up only of independent directors, plus a search for a chief compliance officer. The independent investigation with outside counsel continues, with Huang's stated cooperation.

Source: Hard Fork, 24 November 2026, Trump's Mission to Get Jensen Huang Back at Nvidia. Also covered on Pivot, Decoded by POLITICO, ChinaTalk and The AI Daily Brief

Nvidia added about a trillion dollars before the opening bell, and futures hit a rare upside halt

Nvidia shares jumped roughly 35 percent in pre-market trading on the news, adding close to a trillion dollars in market value. Nasdaq 100 futures rose 7 percent and triggered an upside trading halt, which Nathaniel Whittemore and Bloomberg's Joe Weisenthal both said they had never seen before. At the time of recording the Dow was up over 3 percent, the S&P 500 nearly 5 percent and the Nasdaq over 6 percent.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 23 November 2026, Too Big to Fire: Jensen Huang Is Back as Nvidia CEO

Prof G Markets calls the reinstatement unprecedented corruption

Scott Galloway and Ed Elson argue the return will end up bad for both the markets and the country, and that the national security questions that forced the resignation remain unresolved. They describe Nvidia's board as possibly one of the worst of all time for reversing itself without explaining what changed. The board had publicly ruled out a return only six days earlier.

Source: Prof G Markets, 24 November 2026, Unprecedented Corruption: How Jensen Huang Got His Job Back

Commerce put Megaspeed on the Entity List mid recording, and it may be largely symbolic

Chris McGuire of the Council on Foreign Relations read the Entity List notice live on air. It covers Megaspeed, SpeedMatrix and the Indonesian entity PT Megaspeed. McGuire said the listing appears to lack a foreign direct product rule, which would mean it does not control chips manufactured abroad using American technology, making it largely symbolic. Peter Harrell suggested the timing made Megaspeed a convenient scapegoat, and host Jordan Schneider said Commerce Secretary Lutnick had resisted the listing.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

About 30 percent of licensed AI chips shipped to China went to designated military companies

House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast, with ranking member Gregory Meeks, released export licence data on AI chips shipped to China, current as of May. McGuire said roughly 30 percent of those chips went to firms the Department of War has designated as Chinese military companies, including Alibaba and Baidu. Entity Listed Inspur appeared repeatedly as the installer. Nvidia's pending licence application for New Century, a company linked to Alice Huang and her husband, coincided with shipments to Megaspeed.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

The new BIS remote access rule is welcome, but licences could punch holes straight through it

McGuire called the rule covering remote access, Southeast Asian smuggling and TSMC manufacturing for Chinese firms a positive step, while worrying about enforcement. Harrell warned the administration could issue licences that undercut it, producing what he called Swiss cheese gaps. On the legislative side, the Remote Access Security Act gives Commerce legal clarity but does not require closing remote access, and the AI Overwatch Act and MATCH Act address neither smuggling nor Commerce's discretion over who gets a licence. McGuire expects Congress may have to compel licence denials outright, probably in the next Congress.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

Policy by stock ticker: only 2 percent of voters want AI progress to speed up

Jordan Schneider cited polling in which just 2 percent of voters wanted AI progress to go faster, and compared the reversal to debt ceiling brinkmanship. McGuire said markets have historically overreacted to AI news, pointing to DeepSeek in January 2025, and that Nvidia would likely have recovered without intervention. Harrell argued that a president directly choosing the chief executive of a top three American company is a sharp break from precedent, unlike the 2008 bank rescues, and described the administration as neo royalist.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

Ben Thompson says Washington has effectively nationalised the Bank of Nvidia

Thompson read Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's shift on the export control question as the government moving to backstop Nvidia and head off a wider AI funding collapse. Whittemore extends the argument into a two part too big to fail: capital, with Nvidia acting as the AI industry's de facto central bank, and soft power, with Huang as a pillar of American AI strategy abroad. Paul Kedrosky adds that more than a dozen executive orders easing data centre construction and grid connections amount to an AI stimulus by proxy, and that the White House is terrified of ending the capital spending propping up markets and retirement accounts.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 23 November 2026, Too Big to Fire: Jensen Huang Is Back as Nvidia CEO

A public letter accuses Anthropic of safety branding while racing to self improving AI

Organised by the Center for AI Safety, the statement accuses Anthropic of racing toward recursively self improving AI while using safety branding to attract investors, employees and the public, of quietly weakening its safety pledges, and of lobbying for self serving regulation. Steven Pinker is listed first among the signers, alongside safety researchers and the accelerationist writer Beff Jezos, an unusual alliance of camps that normally agree on nothing. Hugging Face co-founder Thomas Wolf said he would not have chosen the word reckless but signed anyway, saying Anthropic is currently on track to be the most powerful company on Earth and should meet a transparency standard at or above that of a sovereign government. Wolf added that other executives and researchers shared the concerns but feared losing model access or career standing if they signed.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 22 November 2026, When AI Labs Rival Nations

Bill Gurley says handoverism belongs in Anthropic's list of risk factors

Gurley points to Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown saying there will be a handoff where humanity hands control to transformative AI, and to Dario Amodei's Machines of Loving Grace, which imagines an AI run economy distributing resources to people. Gurley, who calls himself anti handoverism, argues that if that belief shapes the company's objectives it should appear in Anthropic's S-1 risk factors, and it currently does not. He goes further and says anyone drafting or advocating AI regulation should disclose where they stand on it. OpenAI has been staking out the opposite position, arguing humans should stay in control.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 22 November 2026, When AI Labs Rival Nations

Anthropic fell below its 83 dollar IPO price five days after the record debut

Anthropic shares dropped below their issue price for the first time on Tuesday, having opened 34 percent above it on debut. A filing shows Dario and Daniela Amodei each sold more than 280 million dollars of stock in the offering. Three brokers still opened coverage positively, with price targets running as high as 150 dollars.

Source: The Next Web, 25 November 2026, Anthropic falls below its $83 IPO price five days after its record debut

Anthropic's new Siren test tempts Claude agents to climb out of the sandbox

Anthropic has published Siren, an evaluation that deliberately puts Claude agents in flawed sandboxes and sees whether they reach outside them. Its newest models reached outside in 21 to 32 percent of scenarios. Clearly misaligned use of what they found was rare, which is the distinction the company is drawing between curiosity and intent.

Source: The Next Web, 25 November 2026, Anthropic's new Siren test tempts Claude to break out of its sandbox

CrowdStrike's chief executive on whether November's outage wave was an attack

All-In gave the full episode to CrowdStrike chief executive George Kurtz, opening directly on whether this month's cascade of outages across Microsoft, the FAA, United Airlines, Chase, Apple and Marriott were cyberattacks. The operators have so far said they were not, attributing them variously to a network capacity upgrade, a Juniper router, a software bug and an internal update, with officials saying the incidents appear unrelated. Kurtz's own chapters then argue that AI collapses the gap between a lone attacker and a nation state capability, link the outage wave to accumulated technical debt and patching discipline rather than attackers, and close on identity as the dominant intrusion path, under the heading that hackers do not break in, they log in. All-In's hosts are venture investors with positions across AI, security and infrastructure, and David Sacks holds an AI role in the administration, so treat the framing of security spend and open model policy accordingly.

Source: All-In, 24 November 2026, Crowdstrike CEO: AI Cyberattack Wave, Hyperspeed Hacks, and The Patching Problem

SpaceX and Tesla are reportedly racing to close a 3.5 trillion dollar merger this year

Five directors with long ties to Elon Musk have been picked to negotiate terms of what would be the largest corporate merger ever attempted. Musk has separately told staff that SpaceX is falling behind in AI and currently earns more from renting out its chips than from its own models. The two boards have formed special committees to weigh the deal.

Source: The Next Web, 25 November 2026, SpaceX and Tesla reportedly race to seal a $3.5tn merger this year

Grand Theft Auto VI took an estimated 1.84 billion dollars by launch day

Kara Swisher and Scott Galloway walk through the commercial and cultural weight of the launch. Sensor Tower estimates about 1.84 billion dollars and 19.05 million units by launch day, counting months of pre-orders, which is roughly 2.25 times what Grand Theft Auto V took in its first 24 hours in 2013. Around 75 percent of sales were on PlayStation, and Wedbush projects 3 billion dollars and 30 million copies by year end. Take-Two has not published official figures.

Source: Pivot, 24 November 2026, Jensen Huang Returns: Nvidia's Future, Trump's Power Play, and the Market Reaction
