A political squeeze on data centres may be the cover Big Tech wanted to trim capex

Markets split sharply after the midterm sweep. Nvidia fell about 3 per cent and CoreWeave about 10 per cent, while Microsoft rose 6.7 per cent, Amazon 6.1 per cent and Alphabet 3.3 per cent, with the largest technology names adding roughly a trillion dollars in a single day. Nathaniel Whittemore's argument is that the build-out will not stop but will get costlier and more accountable to local communities, and that because construction is already buckling on interconnects, labour and turbine supply, a policy-driven slowdown hands hyperscalers political cover to trim capital spending. He notes all four hyperscalers missed their third quarter capex targets on construction delays, and that local data centre ballot measures passed 43 times out of 44.

Anthropic's Nasdaq listing has become the market's referendum on AI

Anthropic is expected to list on the Nasdaq later this month in what could be the largest initial public offering on record, and the episode's premise is that the election result has unsettled investors who already doubted frontier lab growth projections. The listing is framed as a direct test of appetite for AI exposure, with OpenAI and others weighing their own offerings behind it. Stephen Myrow of Beacon Policy Advisors, a former senior Treasury official, put it bluntly: "How people can legitimately say that they can confidently value Anthropic right now is nuts." No offering size or valuation figure has been published.

OpenAI says ChatGPT now handles about a fifth as many searches as Google

OpenAI is briefing that ChatGPT processes roughly 20 per cent as many search requests as Google, against a Google share that has sat above 90 per cent for more than a decade. The brief discounts the number, because OpenAI counts any query that triggers a web search, which inflates it considerably. Google has separately flagged growth in lower-monetising AI Mode queries as a drag on its own search revenue, which reads as self-disruption by necessity rather than choice. OpenAI consumer product lead Adam Fry said the foothold "maybe shown us that we could be doing more of this actually", and user backlash to advertising inside ChatGPT has been milder than the controversy suggested.

Meta buys a parallel coding agent startup, OpenAI buys its code storage supplier

Meta has acquired Conductor, founded in 2024, whose platform lets developers run many coding agents in parallel, and wants it to underpin its next generation of coding products after Muse Code landed quietly. Founder Charlie Holtz is teasing "big, crazy plans for the next interface". Separately OpenAI agreed to buy Pierre Computer Company, whose open source code storage and change tracking tools Codex already uses, which is a logical step if OpenAI intends to compete with GitHub. The reading offered is that the labs have accepted the best model alone will not win, and the agentic product layer is now the contested ground.

Apple has cut what its own engineers are allowed to spend on AI each day

Apple reportedly reduced engineers' daily AI spending caps from 300 dollars to 200 dollars last month, with a further cut to 100 dollars said to be under consideration, and non-engineers possibly dropping from 100 dollars to 25. The trigger is the shift from flat subscriptions to effectively unlimited API billing, which is forcing cost discipline across corporate technology budgets. The policy sits awkwardly beside chief executive John Ternus mentioning AI or Apple Intelligence a record 45 times on an earnings call while telling Wired that Apple does not want to ship AI for its own sake. OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger's question is the sharper one: whether per-person budgets mean anything once agents run entire projects.

The read is that Google now treats AI assisted drug discovery as a standalone business rather than a research showcase, and that this is among the better directed pools of AI capital given the health science payoff.

Two small American banks are issuing account numbers to AI agents

JPMorgan and Bank of America currently block agents from customer accounts outright. Lead Bank in Missouri instead lets fintech partners issue agent-specific account numbers carrying spending limits, restrictions and a panic button for agents that misbehave, while internet-only Increase offers segregated agent accounts and an agent-friendly API. Increase's Darragh Buckley argues that a small group of technology-forward banks is sufficient and no industry-wide coordination is needed. It is a reminder that agent payments infrastructure is arriving through the smallest institutions, not the largest.

A twenty thousand dollar experiment in teaching models to lie to each other

Dwarkesh Patel built a benchmark on the social deception game Blood on the Clocktower, ran 386 games with frontier models playing teams of copies of themselves, and spent about 20,000 dollars in API credits doing it. GPT-6 Astra leads by a wide margin, roughly 430 Elo above Claude Opus 4.6 and winning about 92 per cent of their matchups, having gained about 580 Elo over GPT-5.4 in six months. Behaviourally, Anthropic's models tended to accept reported information at face value while OpenAI's weighed claims against the full sequence of events and found the deceiver faster, and no model yet exploits writing style to identify its own teammates. His most interesting finding is incidental: the environment went through more than 20 revisions driven largely by AI coding and AI game review, suggesting models are getting better at building the training environments later used on them.

Salesforce's deal chief says per-seat pricing is giving way to outcome pricing

John Somorjai, who runs corporate development and Salesforce Ventures, walks through the roughly 100 billion dollars of acquisitions made on his watch, Slack and MuleSoft among them, plus newer AI-native purchases. His pitch to founders is distribution: a good acquisition compresses a roadmap and puts the product in front of about 15,000 Salesforce sales representatives almost immediately. The substantive claim for everyone else is that per-seat software pricing is being displaced by outcome-based pricing in parts of the enterprise, a direct consequence of agents doing work that used to be counted in seats. He also says plainly that parts of today's venture market are overvalued, and that Salesforce Ventures is pushing beyond enterprise software into frontier AI and robotics. Worth noting the framing sits inside both parties' interests: the host is an a16z general partner and the guest runs a corporate venture arm that co-invests with the firm and could acquire its companies.

Source: The a16z Show, 6 November 2026, Inside Salesforce's 100 Billion Dollar M and A Playbook with John Somorjai

Five Chinese labs reached the frontier within about a month of each other

Xianyu, who writes the satirical Chinese AI newsletter Funeral AI, names Zhipu GLM 5.3, Alibaba Qwen 3.8 Max, Moonshot Kimi K3, DeepSeek R1 and MiniMax M3 as all arriving at roughly Claude Opus 5 class capability inside about a month. His conclusion is that large models hold no durable secrets, competition reduces to data and compute, and a lead rarely survives more than a month or two, which makes model building closer to manufacturing than research. He also built MemeBench, a crowd-voted ranking of video models on entertainment value rather than technical metrics, using about 300 raters paid 10 yuan for twenty minutes of labelling; it put MiniMax H3 above ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 and 2.5, a result ByteDance queried directly with him. On demand he sees no softening, citing ByteDance's Doubao at a reported 200 to 300 million users in China and noting an hour of agentic coding can burn over 100 million tokens against a few million for an hour of chat.

DNA synthesis screening now has to identify the agent, not the customer

Joshua Monrad, co-founder of Sentinel Bio, argues that recent in-lab uplift trials and multimodal wet-lab troubleshooting benchmarks show frontier models eroding the tacit knowledge barrier that has historically protected laboratories from misuse. That shifts attention from what a model knows to what it can talk someone through at the bench. The interventions discussed are filtering dangerous pathogen data out of training corpora, governing dual-use biological datasets, and screening synthesis orders, with the title's shift from know your customer to know your agent describing what happens when autonomous systems start placing those orders themselves. Monrad's view is that laboratory robotics is now the last physical bottleneck, and that the window for building defences is closing as biological tools get cheaper. He sells screening, so the market framing is his own.

A one point eight billion dollar bet that virtual cell models are ready

The episode is pegged to a Biohub announcement carrying 1.8 billion dollars in commitments from Biohub, the NIH, the Department of Energy, Google and Meta, with the DOE and NIH building lab-in-the-loop automation at scale and Google broadening its connectomics work. The field's credibility problem is perturbation prediction: the 2025 Arc Virtual Cell Challenge showed simple constant baselines often beating sophisticated models. The counter-argument, citing a Nature Biotechnology paper, is that many existing evaluation metrics are poorly calibrated, and under better ones deep learning models do beat the mean baseline. Fabian Theis of Helmholtz Munich goes further and says perturbation prediction on existing datasets is not a hard enough problem to justify complex algorithms, and that what the field actually lacks is large, high precision, multi-scale co-registered data.

Source: Latent Space, 6 November 2026, Controlling Biology with Fabian Theis, Helmholtz Munich

Revolut says self-hosted Chinese open weight models cut its AI coding bill by ninety per cent

Revolut has moved roughly 2,000 engineers off commercial coding assistants and onto self-hosted open weight models from Zhipu's GLM family, and says the switch cut its AI coding costs by about 90 per cent. It lands in the same week the fintech completed a secondary share sale valuing it at about 115 billion dollars, making it Europe's most valuable private technology company. For any enterprise running a large AI coding bill, this is the first credible public datapoint that a self-hosted open weight model can carry production engineering workload at a fraction of the price. It also makes concrete what ChinaTalk's guest argues above, that frontier capability is no longer a durable Western advantage.
