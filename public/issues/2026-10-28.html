Anthropic files to list on Nasdaq and three shows lead with the same story

Anthropic's public S-1 landed on Monday morning US time and became the lead item on Pivot, Prof G Markets and The AI Daily Brief within hours. The filing sets out revenue of 16.3 billion dollars for the first half of 2026, against 41 million dollars for the whole of 2023, with second quarter revenue of 11.6 billion. Kara Swisher and Scott Galloway worked through the revenue, the losses and what the filing says about risk, while the reported plan is a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Roughly 48 percent of first half revenue now arrives through the Bedrock, Vertex and Azure marketplaces rather than direct.

Source: Pivot, 27 October 2026, Anthropic's IPO Filing, Elon's Talarico Obsession, and Elizabeth Holmes' Box Office Surprise

The catch in the filing is the compute Anthropic has already promised to buy

Ed Elson put Anthropic's S-1 to Paul Kedrosky and framed the episode around what the title calls an 800 billion dollar catch. The filing discloses non-cancelable compute commitments running into the next decade, reported elsewhere at up to 750 billion dollars, including cloud deals with Google, Amazon and Microsoft and up to 120.5 billion dollars of Nvidia powered capacity. The obligations hold whether or not the capacity gets used, which is the part Kedrosky argues investors are skating past. Anthropic holds about 132 billion dollars in cash and short term investments against them.

Source: Prof G Markets, 27 October 2026, The $800 Billion Catch Behind Anthropic's IPO

Anthropic turned its first profitable quarter just before filing

Latent Space opened its Anthropic episode with a newly recorded segment on the S-1. Revenue per employee has risen to about 6.6 million dollars from about 1.4 million a year earlier, and the company reports its first quarter of GAAP profitability at a margin near 19 percent, a swing from deeply negative a year ago. Gross margin for the first half came in at 85 percent against 12 percent for the same period in 2025. The filing also discloses a delay in pre-training a new frontier model.

Source: Latent Space, 27 October 2026, How Anthropic ships Claude with Claude: Separating the Brain from the Hands

The Messages API has been rewritten in Rust and the rollout is finished

Katelyn Lesse and Angela Jiang from Anthropic's Claude Platform team said the port of the Messages API from Python to Rust completed its rollout last week. They put the gains at roughly 40 to 50 percent lower CPU usage and 10 to 20 percent better time to first token, describing both as ballpark figures. The migration was validated by running shadow traffic through the Rust and Python stacks in parallel and diffing the outputs rather than cutting over and watching dashboards.

Source: Latent Space, 27 October 2026, How Anthropic ships Claude with Claude: Separating the Brain from the Hands

Opus 5.5 was deliberately made harder to distil from

The team said Opus 5.5 costs less to run than its predecessor and was tuned for cleaner writing after feedback that Opus 5 wrote in a convoluted way. They also made the API harder to distil from, chiefly by requiring preserved thinking blocks to be carried across turns, and conceded the user experience trade-offs that came with it. Because developers now have to carry those blocks, Anthropic shipped a compaction feature in the API so teams do not have to build their own. Trust grants, an authorisation model governing which users can reach which models, shipped alongside it.

Source: Latent Space, 27 October 2026, How Anthropic ships Claude with Claude: Separating the Brain from the Hands

Anthropic has split the agent brain from the hands

In Claude's managed agents architecture a durable harness and session log, the brain, is decoupled from the sandboxes that execute code, the hands. Anthropic says the split improves latency and reliability and means a sandbox failure no longer kills the agent run. Credentials sit in a separate vault and are injected through an egress proxy, so the agent itself only ever sees placeholders. The team also described a memory feature they call dreaming, where one managed agent inspects another agent's prior work and writes memories back, and said they plan to expose multi-agent strategy primitives so developers can build their own harness logic.

Source: Latent Space, 27 October 2026, How Anthropic ships Claude with Claude: Separating the Brain from the Hands

Researchers say they reached Anthropic's internal systems in under 12 hours

Cathedral, a months old cybersecurity start-up founded by four former DOGE staffers, told Anthropic that its researchers had penetrated sensitive internal data and messaging systems at the company, according to a draft report reviewed by The Washington Post. The work was done with AI agents and the reported time to access was less than twelve hours. Cathedral raised 160 million dollars at a 1.4 billion dollar valuation in July, in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz and Sequoia. The story was flipped into the Digital News magazine and sits oddly against the security language in the same week's IPO filing.

Source: The Washington Post, 26 October 2026, Researchers at cybersecurity start-up Cathedral got inside Anthropic

The AI Daily Brief weighs the filing against what the growth would have to be

Nathaniel Whittemore's read of the S-1 centres on the gap between the roughly 132 billion dollars of cash on hand and the hundreds of billions of dollars of compute Anthropic has committed to buy. His framing question is plain: how much future growth has to arrive to make commitments on that scale look sane rather than reckless. He treats the strong revenue line as settled and the obligations as the open question.

He compared Duet with ChatGPT's Dots and asked whether proactive personal agents, the kind that act without being asked each time, have become genuinely useful or remain a demo. The verdict matters more for buyers than another benchmark does, because it is about whether anyone changes their working day.

Musk acknowledges he restricted Ukraine's access to Starlink

Pivot covered Musk's admission that he limited Ukrainian access to Starlink, a question that had been circling since 2023 without a clear answer from him. Zelenskyy has said the restrictions applied inside Ukraine and that SpaceX was not responding to Kyiv's inquiries. The underlying reporting is thin and partly from low quality outlets, so the detail is worth treating carefully, but the admission itself is the point: a single commercial operator can switch a country's battlefield communications on or off.

The useful story about China's economy is the failures, not the champions

Harvard Business School's Meg Rithmire told ChinaTalk that analysts learn more from the collapsed conglomerates than from the national champions. Anbang, HNA and Wanda all raised capital on public exchanges and moved it through thousands of subsidiaries, and that opaque, distressed part of the system is where the mechanics show. Her argument is that the zombie firm pattern has not gone away but has migrated from real estate into state directed industrial policy sectors.

Source: ChinaTalk, 27 October 2026, What's Broken About China's Economy and The Cost of Keeping China Out, Meg Rithmire

Rithmire argues Washington should let BYD and CATL build in America

In the same conversation Rithmire makes the case that total exclusion of Chinese firms carries its own cost to the United States, and that selected companies should be allowed to build on American soil under strict conditions. She names BYD and CATL as the candidates, tied to a recent Foreign Affairs piece. The argument cuts against the direction of travel in Washington, which is why it is worth hearing stated plainly.

Source: ChinaTalk, 27 October 2026, What's Broken About China's Economy and The Cost of Keeping China Out, Meg Rithmire
