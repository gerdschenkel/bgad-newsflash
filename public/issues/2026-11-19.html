OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and the developers who tried it first are not impressed

OpenAI put GPT-6.2 Astra and the cheaper Sol variant into ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API this week, and the earliest reaction from developers has been sour. Testers report that the model stops halfway through coding projects and cuts its writing so short that it is hard to follow. The counterweight the episode cites is price: a 35 per cent fall in cost per task on one benchmark. The question Whittemore puts is whether OpenAI has decided that helping office workers with routine tasks matters more than keeping developers happy.

Nvidia's board rules out bringing Jensen Huang back

The board has declined to reinstate Huang, shutting the fastest route back for a founder who resigned less than two weeks ago while the Megaspeed investigation is still running. It does so against a loud public campaign: Pivot cited a reinstatement petition with more than 180,000 signatures, including Elon Musk, who wrote that Huang deserves due process rather than mob justice. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News the board should look outside the company, comparing the situation to a bank where the regulator would already be removing executives. Scott Galloway put the odds of Huang rejoining even the board at closer to one per cent.

Nvidia reports its first quarter without its founder in the chair

Ed Elson and Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson go through Nvidia's first earnings report since Huang resigned and what it says about the company without him. Luria also takes the succession question head on, arguing who would serve shareholders best as the next chief executive, and gives his reading of where the AI trade now stands. The episode closes on Anthropic's initial public offering.

Nvidia is said to be stepping back from bankrolling the AI build out

Kara Swisher cited a Wall Street Journal report that Huang had been laying the groundwork for Nvidia to retreat from financing the AI sector, and quoted him telling cloud executives in Berlin that he did not need them. Interim chief executive and finance chief Colette Kress had reportedly pushed back on large deals arranged through Huang's son in law. A UBS analyst estimated that a Nvidia pullback could take 50 billion dollars or more out of the market. Galloway noted that vendor financing is nothing new, but taking equity stakes in your own customers goes further.

Source: Pivot, 17 November 2026, Jensen Huang's Future, Bessent vs. Lutnick, and SNL's Big Tech Roast

Commerce keeps Megaspeed off the entity list while the rest of Washington pushes the other way

Despite suspicions that Megaspeed routed Nvidia chips to China, Commerce has not added it to the entity list. Bessent said Treasury, the War Department, the trade representative and State had all favoured listing it for months, but that Commerce holds the pen. Swisher said Commerce has now closed the loophole that let China reach Nvidia compute through data centres in third countries, and that both Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have been called to testify before Congress next month. Galloway claimed chips shipped to Singapore in breach of export controls had grown to roughly 18 per cent of Nvidia's sales.

Source: Pivot, 17 November 2026, Jensen Huang's Future, Bessent vs. Lutnick, and SNL's Big Tech Roast

A bad fortnight for uptime turns into an argument about ageing infrastructure

A nationwide FAA ground stop halted departures across the United States for more than four hours and caused over 9,000 cancellations. Officials said it began during work replacing old copper wiring with fibre, and that the 875 million dollar AI assisted air traffic system rolled out three days earlier was unrelated. Swisher also pointed to a separate Microsoft outage that disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot worldwide for 36 hours. Prof G Markets brought in Sherri Davidoff of LMG Security on what is behind the run of failures, and The AI Daily Brief framed the same wave as a question about the rush to modernise systems that are long past their design life.

Databricks' Ali Ghodsi says the intelligence has arrived and the context has not

Ghodsi argues that AI already has the raw intelligence businesses need, and that the real constraint is feeding it enterprise context: turning meetings, documents, code and org charts into something that surfaces decisions, blockers and performance in real time. He pairs that with a long view on adoption, saying the corporate shift will take decades rather than years, and demonstrates how Databricks uses agents on its own operations. He also makes the case for staying private rather than listing, before turning to recursive self improvement and cyber risk. The All-In hosts are active venture investors, so the enthusiasm for private market staying power is worth reading with that in mind.

Source: All-In, 17 November 2026, Databricks CEO: AGI Is Already Here, Why Companies Are Behind, and the Superintelligence Debate

Companies used a third more AI tokens in October and barely spent more

Businesses tracked by Ramp used 36 per cent more AI tokens in October while their spending rose just 2 per cent. Drawing on Ramp, Databricks and Arcana data, the episode reads that gap as firms switching to cheaper models and widening their use of AI well beyond coding. The laggard is autonomous agents, where adoption is still well behind the general growth in usage.

a16z backs a personal context layer that sits between you and your agents

Ken Liu's First Person Labs is building Second, a user owned vault that keeps a continuously updated model of your preferences, priorities, relationships and goals and exposes it to whichever agents you choose. It reads the operating system event stream on Apple, Microsoft and Linux machines, with optional connectors to Slack, Signal and WhatsApp, and Liu's framing is Batman and Alfred: the butler states the preference without revealing who is buying. He concedes prompt injection is not definitively solvable, and says confidential inference shifts trust to the hardware vendor. Read it as a launch interview: a16z announced its investment in First Person Labs the day before, and the two hosts, Martin Casado and Joel de la Garza, wrote that announcement.

Source: The a16z Show, 18 November 2026, The Missing Layer for Personal AI, First Person Labs' Ken Liu

Alex Imas and Tyler Cowen take up the gap between how quickly AI capability improves and how slowly that shows up in growth, jobs, wages and productivity. One of the threads is whether the next unlock is agents coordinating with each other at scale rather than any single agent getting smarter. It is the counterweight to a week of capability announcements.

Black Forest Labs trains FLUX 3 on pixels, motion and sound at once

Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell argue that one model should learn from stills, video and audio together: stills show where things are, video shows how they got there, and sound reveals impacts the camera missed. Their video upscaler is built on the same FLUX 3 backbone, because per frame sharpening causes shimmer and temporal consistency matters even for a task that simple. On architecture, they put the trade off plainly: autoregressive models learn from every token in a training example at once, while diffusion can be distilled down to a few sampling steps, so the choice depends on whether you are optimising training cost or inference latency.

Source: Latent Space, 16 November 2026, Building Visual Intelligence, Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell, Black Forest Labs
