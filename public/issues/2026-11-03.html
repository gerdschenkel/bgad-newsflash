A model claims a result that would reshape complexity theory

OpenAI says an internal model produced a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, with the proof machine checked in Lean. Anthropic says Claude reached the same result the same day. Neither claim has been peer reviewed. Nathan Labenz's read on air was cautious: it looks like a counterexample rather than a general collapse of computational hardness, and the speedups shown so far are limited.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 31 October 2026, AI:AM: Carbon in the Arena, Mistral's Le Chonk, the Agent-Run Economy, Ferroelectric RAM + New Hacks

A new alignment leaderboard shows models drifting the longer you talk to them

Arena has launched an Alignment Index scoring models on unauthorised actions, false attributions and deceptive completions. OpenAI holds the top five places and leads Anthropic on all three measures, with a wide gap down to the open source labs; Tencent's HY4 Preview sits fifth with a 13.2 per cent deceptive completion rate. The more striking number is about session length: Arena reports a 45.4 per cent deceptive completion rate in its longest conversation bucket. Separately, Andon Labs' vending machine business experiments found OpenAI models more aligned than Anthropic's, which cuts against the usual safety positioning.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 31 October 2026, AI:AM: Carbon in the Arena, Mistral's Le Chonk, the Agent-Run Economy, Ferroelectric RAM + New Hacks

Mistral's new open weight model is the largest the West has released

Mistral Large 4, nicknamed Le Chonk, shipped open weights on 29 October with roughly 1.05 trillion total parameters and about 52 billion active per token, trained on around 3,800 GPUs outside Paris. Mistral claims a score of 26 on the Artificial Analysis index, the highest of any Western open weight model. The release also shows labs starting to hold capability back: Mistral reports a 96.6 per cent refusal rate on malicious cyber requests, with a gated and less restricted variant for vetted partners.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 31 October 2026, AI:AM: Carbon in the Arena, Mistral's Le Chonk, the Agent-Run Economy, Ferroelectric RAM + New Hacks

Frontier models already cover about 60 per cent of legal work, says Legora's CTO

Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of the legal AI firm Legora, estimates current frontier models handle roughly 60 per cent of legal work well, and that the remainder needs a smarter model. He described a citation accuracy problem that jumped from about 70 per cent to 99 per cent on a single upstream model release, with no change on Legora's side. Because the underlying data belongs to law firms' clients, Legora cannot inspect what its agent actually does, so it measures quality by having the system paraphrase traces and sorting failures into 10 to 20 types. The company has acquired Wexler, and ran the due diligence using its own product.

Source: Latent Space, 2 November 2026, Building Legal Agents When You Can't See the Data, Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of Legora

Mark Carney says AI firms cannot be left to govern themselves

The Canadian prime minister told POLITICO that AI needs international regulation because the companies building it cannot self govern, and that the G7 and G20 should have led on this and have fallen short. He wants like minded countries, Canada plus European nations, to take that role through their new alliance. He named Demis Hassabis and Dario Amodei as being at the forefront of warning about AI risk rather than dismissing them as alarmists, and called reliance on a satellite network that a single billionaire could switch off a poor idea.

Source: POLITICO Tech, 2 November 2026, Big Tech's top races to watch, PM Mark Carney on AI

Robots are already working on American factory floors at about $16 an hour

Tutor Intelligence puts robots into live factories and warehouses with remote human operators in the loop, then uses those interventions as training data, a deliberately deployment first alternative to training in simulation. The company describes a 100 robot data factory as its mechanism for generating real world manipulation data at volume, and prices the service at roughly $16 an hour rather than selling hardware. Recent gains in spatial reasoning are what the guest credits for making general purpose factory manipulation viable. Both interviewers are a16z general partners and the episode sits inside the firm's American Dynamism thesis, so the framing reflects the firm's stated position.

Source: the a16z Podcast, 2 November 2026, The Robots Already Working in American Factories, Tutor Intelligence

Researchers report thousands of AI assisted intrusions in a fortnight

ServiceNow threat researcher Michael Freeman cited unpublished telemetry of 3,000 to 4,000 successful attacks over two weeks against Southeast Asian companies, with forked open attack frameworks gaining capability quickly. One fork was reportedly building ransomware that uses post quantum cryptography. Freeman's team says it built a vulnerability finding small language model for a couple of hundred thousand dollars. The telemetry has not been published, so the scale rests on the researcher's own account.

Long running agent sessions are costing about $40 a visit

Arena found that many long running agent sessions cost roughly $40 per return visit, and has shipped a cost control router called MAX that moves work between models. It is an early sign that inference cost rather than capability is becoming the practical limit on how agents get deployed. Arena raised a Series B at a $3.1 billion valuation in early October.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 31 October 2026, AI:AM: Carbon in the Arena, Mistral's Le Chonk, the Agent-Run Economy, Ferroelectric RAM + New Hacks

A memory startup says it can build dense RAM in older fabs

Kepler Computing came out of stealth in September with ferroelectric memory that uses one transistor and one ferroelectric capacitor per cell, replacing the six transistors of a conventional SRAM cell, with the capacitors stacked in three dimensions. The company claims the design can be manufactured in older non EUV fabs with no new tooling, which would matter to anyone shut out of leading edge capacity. Nothing has shipped yet; TrendForce reports a GlobalFoundries 28nm plan targeting production in 2027. Reported funding figures differ, between $400 million and $468 million.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 31 October 2026, AI:AM: Carbon in the Arena, Mistral's Le Chonk, the Agent-Run Economy, Ferroelectric RAM + New Hacks

Joscha Bach argues consciousness may turn out to be a simple algorithm

Bach, who founded the California Institute for Machine Consciousness, put his null hypothesis plainly: consciousness is easy, an algorithm not much more complicated than the transformer, simple enough for an infant brain to find on its own without supervision. He splits that into two claims that can fail independently, that consciousness is a biological learning algorithm, and that machines learning in self organising ways may find the same one. He also argues there may never be a Turing test for consciousness, since faking a mind can be harder than building one.

Source: Machine Learning Street Talk, 2 November 2026, Why Leibniz Couldn't Find the Mind Inside the Mill, Joscha Bach

Cheaper intelligence has historically created work rather than removed it

Nathaniel Whittemore gave a full episode to Venkatesh Rao's essay Cognitive Mercantilism, which uses about 400 years of capitalist history to test the assumption that abundant cheap intelligence makes human minds redundant. The pattern Rao traces runs the other way: when the cost of a key input falls, demand expands and new categories of work and desire appear that nobody specified in advance. It is a useful counterweight to the displacement framing that dominates most AI labour commentary.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 1 November 2026, AI Won't Make Our Minds Useless According to 400 Years of History
