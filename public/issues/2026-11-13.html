Jensen Huang resigns from Nvidia, and the chief financial officer takes over

Nvidia confirmed on 12 November that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, after 33 years at the company he co-founded in 1993. A regulatory filing shows the board accepted the resignation on 11 November. Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim chief executive while keeping the finance job, and Harvey Jones, a director since 1993 and a former Synopsys chief executive, becomes Nvidia's first ever chair of the board. The company says it will weigh internal and external candidates, and it reports third quarter results on 17 November, which will be the first test of whether guidance set under Huang still holds.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 12 November 2026, Jensen Huang Is Out. Everything in AI Is Now Up for Grabs.

What brought him down: an affair, an alleged Chinese agent and a board inquiry

Kevin Roose and Casey Newton recorded with Stephen Witt, Huang's biographer, two days before the resignation, when the story was still about an affair. Huang emailed staff on 8 November admitting he had been unfaithful to his wife. The woman, who goes by Alice Huang, was a managing director of the Singapore registered data centre firm Megaspeed, and United States intelligence reportedly concluded she was passing information to Beijing. Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation that same day with outside counsel and said it had no reason to believe national security or trade secrets were compromised. Reuters then reported a second relationship, with an Nvidia executive, and the board met late on 11 November. Alice Huang has not been charged and has not commented, and the espionage assessment rests on unnamed officials.

Source: Hard Fork, 10 November 2026, The Jensen Huang Affair

The market punished Nvidia and paid its rivals

Shares fell as much as 14 per cent on 12 November before recovering to about 166 dollars, down roughly 4 per cent on the day, with more than a trillion dollars of value gone this month and the stock about 35 per cent below its high. RBC Capital Markets cut its target from 330 dollars to 230 dollars. Broadcom rose 4.8 per cent and AMD 2.1 per cent, and the Chinese chipmakers Moore Threads, MetaX and Cambricon each jumped close to 10 per cent. Nvidia's own customers went the other way, with CoreWeave down 9.5 per cent and Nebius down 18.4 per cent. Mark Malek of Siebert Financial said the market may be confusing the value of Jensen Huang with the value of Nvidia.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 12 November 2026, Jensen Huang Is Out. Everything in AI Is Now Up for Grabs.

The bigger exposure is the 164.5 billion dollars Nvidia has promised

Nvidia's most recent accounts list 164.5 billion dollars of financial commitments, including an obligation tied to an OpenAI data centre in Ohio capped at 105 billion dollars and more than 50 billion dollars of lease and capacity guarantees. In August the company joined Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone and three other institutions on compute financing platforms aiming at more than 500 billion dollars of third party capital, with Huang saying Nvidia could backstop up to 125 billion dollars of potential deals. Traders have taken to calling it the Bank of AI. The open question is whether that web of interlocking commitments made the buildout sturdier or more fragile, now that the person who signed most of it has gone.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 12 November 2026, Jensen Huang Is Out. Everything in AI Is Now Up for Grabs.

Chinese firms race to book Nvidia capacity offshore before Washington shuts the door

Chinese companies are reportedly rushing to reserve Nvidia compute through third party clouds abroad, the remote access loophole, expecting controls to tighten after the scandal. The Information reports that the Commerce Department is finalising a rule requiring licences before cloud providers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Brazil, Indonesia, Norway and South Africa can receive Blackwell or newer chips, with an exemption for United States operated sites that block remote access from restricted countries. The New York Times reports Commerce halted a months long Megaspeed investigation after contact from Nvidia's general counsel, and Axios reports that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confronted Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over it, which Commerce denies. Megaspeed denies funnelling chips to China.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 12 November 2026, Jensen Huang Is Out. Everything in AI Is Now Up for Grabs.

Washington cleared about two dozen Chinese buyers for H200 chips

Ed Elson gave the first eighteen minutes to Chris McGuire, a former United States official on China and emerging technology, now at the Council on Foreign Relations, under the blunt framing of whether Nvidia broke export rules and why the government may have looked away. The reporting behind the segment: the Bureau of Industry and Security approved about two dozen Chinese companies to buy or handle H200 chips, some with vacant or shell addresses tied to blacklisted firms, others military contractors. The policy path ran from easing H20 controls in August 2025 in exchange for 15 per cent of China revenue to allowing H200 sales in December 2025 for 25 per cent. Nvidia's spokesman said it makes no sense for the Chinese military to depend on American technology.

Source: Prof G Markets, 12 November 2026, The Trillion Dollar Cover Up: What Washington Knew About Jensen Huang

Enterprise AI spending hit a record in October and almost stopped growing

Ara Kharazian, lead economist at Ramp, brought October figures drawn from payments by more than 85,000 United States companies. Corporate AI spending reached a new high but grew only 1.65 per cent month on month, and 56.8 per cent of companies now pay for AI services. Anthropic adoption reached 44.8 per cent, up 1.1 points, while OpenAI held flat at 40.6 per cent. The distribution is extreme: the top 1 per cent of companies spend a median of 10,200 dollars per employee per month, the typical company spends 16.32 dollars, and 1 per cent of customers produce 80 per cent of revenue for both labs. Ramp cautions that its customer base skews larger and more technology forward than the economy.

Source: Prof G Markets, 12 November 2026, The Trillion Dollar Cover Up: What Washington Knew About Jensen Huang

AI budgets have finally escaped the engineering department

In the same Ramp data, customer support and customer success went from 0.8 per cent of tracked AI spending in June to 13.3 per cent in October, a sixteenfold shift in share in four months. Software development remains the largest line at 72 per cent, down from its summer high. The most expensive model tiers accounted for 69.5 per cent of all spending. For anyone selling AI into large organisations, this is the first large payments dataset showing budgets move beyond engineering at scale, and it points at where the next buying centre sits.

Source: Prof G Markets, 12 November 2026, The Trillion Dollar Cover Up: What Washington Knew About Jensen Huang

Palantir's Alex Karp says he is open to funding Democrats in 2028

Karp told POLITICO that Democrats have corrected enough to win his support back, with one condition: not a candidate from the Democratic Socialists of America. He called the Republican midterm losses a referendum on economic justice, said he has been a Democrat his whole life, and described Palantir's products as essentially progressive. The company has hired Jon Tester, the former Montana senator, as an adviser, a role Tester describes as advisory rather than lobbying, ahead of a Democratic Congress in January. Senator Ron Wyden and thirty other Democrats have already pressed Palantir on its Homeland Security contracts and may use compulsory process next year if the company does not cooperate.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, 12 November 2026, Palantir's Alex Karp on welcoming a Democratic Congress

Data centre backlash reaches the mayor's office in Denver

Denver mayor Mike Johnston joined Kara Swisher and Scott Galloway, recorded live in Denver on 8 November, to talk about local opposition to data centres. The city passed a one year moratorium in May after a 180,000 square foot CoreSite facility opened opposite a park in the underserved Globeville and Elyria Swansea area, and named a 26 member working group in October to write permanent rules. Johnston has not ruled out a permanent ban and points to utility bills and water supply in a drought prone city. More than a dozen Colorado communities have passed or are weighing moratoriums, Longmont banned large data centres outright, and a Denver7 database counts close to 500 United States communities that have paused or blocked data centre growth.

Source: Pivot, 10 November 2026, Nvidia CEO's Spy Scandal, JD Vance's 2028 Problem, and America's Brain Drain

Axon says it moves more video every day than YouTube

Rick Smith, founder and chief executive of Axon, said more than a million officers each upload roughly two hours of body camera footage a day, which he claims puts the company's daily video volume above YouTube's. It is his own assertion, with no methodology offered, but it reframes Axon from a hardware vendor into one of the largest private video ingest and processing operations anywhere. Axon now holds about 37 per cent of the drone maker Skydio after a 1.2 billion dollar investment and is its exclusive public safety distributor in the United States and Canada, and Smith expects millions of policing drones within a few years, with any decision to use force made by humans under multi key approval. Treat the whole conversation as a friendly founder interview: the hosts are active investors in this category.

Source: All-In, 10 November 2026, Freedom vs. Safety: Axon CEO Rick Smith on the Future of Policing

Isomorphic Labs puts longevity on the drug design agenda

Max Jaderberg, now president of Alphabet's Isomorphic Labs, told Elad Gil that AI driven molecule optimisation could extend both lifespan and healthspan, not only treat named diseases. His strategic bet is one general drug design engine that works across disease areas, target classes and modalities rather than disease specific teams, with in silico models replacing animal testing and multidrug combinations designed computationally for complex biological pathways. The company has partnerships with Novartis, Eli Lilly and Johnson and Johnson, and still has no candidate in human trials after a 2025 target slipped to the end of 2026.

Source: No Priors, 12 November 2026, Powering AI-Driven Drug Discovery with President of Isomorphic Labs Max Jaderberg

American vendors are selling half a billion dollars of training environments to Chinese labs

ChinaTalk reports that United States data firms including Mercor, Surge AI, AfterQuery and Turing are selling reinforcement learning environments, simulated worlds packaged with grading rubrics, to Chinese labs including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Moonshot. Why it matters: rollouts consume more than 70 per cent of reinforcement learning post training time, so buying ready made environments lets memory constrained Chinese labs skip the most compute hungry part of the work. AfterQuery is reported to draw about half of its roughly 100 million dollar annual revenue from Chinese business. Almost none of this falls under export controls, and the authors want the Bureau of Industry and Security to treat dual use environments under existing rules. This is a written analysis rather than an episode.

Source: ChinaTalk, 11 November 2026, Piggybacking to the Frontier

IBM plans a 1,040 qubit machine by stitching two processors together

IBM says its Nighthawk r3 processor runs 520 qubits and supports 10,000 two qubit gate operations, and that it will link two of them into a single 1,040 qubit system in 2027. The interesting part is the method rather than the number: scaling moves from one ever larger chip to connected modules, the same shift that reshaped classical computing. IBM frames it as the route to the larger fault tolerant machines further out on its roadmap.

Source: IBM Newsroom, 11 November 2026, IBM Quantum Nighthawk Demonstrates New Levels of Speed and Quality

Anthropic adds Sheets, Whiteboards and Meetings

Anthropic said on 10 November that it is extending Claude into three more office tools. Claude Sheets is a spreadsheet where teams work alongside Claude agents on data, formulas and multiple tabs. Claude Whiteboards is a brainstorming surface with summarising built in. Claude Meetings, described as coming soon, takes notes on calls and updates company documents. Sheets and Whiteboards are in beta for subscribers. The pitch is shared context carried across the tools rather than a better spreadsheet, which puts Anthropic against Microsoft and Google rather than beside them, and it lands nine days before a reported public listing.

Source: Gizmodo, 10 November 2026, Anthropic Goes for Microsoft's Crown With New Claude Powered Office Tools
