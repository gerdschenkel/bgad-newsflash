Eighty nine researchers put their names to a statement that Anthropic is not a safe AI company

A statement organised by the Center for AI Safety, published days before the IPO, accuses Anthropic of racing to build recursively self improving AI while using safety branding to court investors, employees and regulators. Signatories include Nobel and Turing laureates alongside co founders of Hugging Face and xAI. Hugging Face co founder Thomas Wolf said he signed despite disagreeing with some of the wording, arguing that a company on track to be among the most powerful on earth should meet transparency standards closer to those of a government than a startup. He added that others share the concerns privately but fear losing model access or damaging their careers.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 22 November 2026, When AI Labs Rival Nations

Handoverism becomes a named position, and Bill Gurley says it belongs in Anthropic's risk disclosures

The debate over how much direction humans keep has acquired a label: handoverism, the view that humanity survives while entrusting the course of its future entirely to AI. Bill Gurley is urging people to learn the term, pointing to Anthropic co founder Tom Brown on an eventual handoff of control and to Dario Amodei's Machines of Loving Grace, which describes an AI run economy distributing resources to humans. Gurley's sharper point is procedural rather than philosophical: if that is the destination, it should appear in the risk disclosures of a newly listed company. Host Nathaniel Whittemore declares himself against the position and argues anyone shaping AI regulation should state where they stand on it.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 22 November 2026, When AI Labs Rival Nations

Estonia's former government CIO says agents are already filing paperwork, and CAPTCHAs are the wrong answer

Luukas Ilves, co founder of The Agentic State and previously Estonia's government CIO, tells ChinaTalk that AI agents have started submitting records requests, grant applications and permit filings at volume. His argument is that the reflex response, adding more CAPTCHAs and friction, is precisely wrong, and that the target should instead be a building permit issued in about a minute. He names Ukraine, Estonia and Singapore as the governments already deploying agents, and argues automation may be what keeps public services running as a large cohort of civil servants retires. He also claims US states are ahead of the federal government, and that one citizen with a coding agent can out build the government serving them.

Source: ChinaTalk, 22 November 2026, The Agentic State

Google ships Gemini 4 Neon, its fourth frontier class model in about two months

Neon follows Argon, Carbon and Hydrogen, an unusually fast cadence even by current standards, and Google is pitching it on speed as much as raw capability. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scored 61, one point ahead of Carbon and within a few points of the leading models from OpenAI and Anthropic, while leading its class on the coding agent index. It uses roughly 15 percent more tokens per task than Carbon but runs about 40 percent faster, and sits mid range on price at three dollars per million tokens. Whittemore's verdict is that its value is offering a genuinely different perspective rather than being better at everything, and that Google's agent and coding harnesses still trail its rivals.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 20 November 2026, Why You Should Try Gemini Neon

Washington closes the third country loophole on Chinese access to AI chips

The Commerce Department issued a rule stopping Chinese companies reaching advanced chips through data centres in other countries, the workaround that had taken much of the sting out of earlier export controls. Tencent president Martin Lau warned of possible model release delays and a pivot to domestic silicon, while Alibaba said the rule would hit next year's products and that it is increasing use of its own chips. The concrete fallout came quickly: Moonshot cancelled a 150 million dollar arrangement for Nvidia chip access through an Indonesian data centre. Southeast Asian data centre operators that had built capacity on Chinese demand are the collateral damage.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 20 November 2026, Why You Should Try Gemini Neon

Nvidia halts H200 sales to China and says the board will not bring Jensen Huang back

Interim chief executive Colette Kress confirmed that Nvidia has stopped H200 shipments to China and that the board will not reinstate Huang, who resigned earlier this month. She also said the company would be more disciplined and selective about financing new cloud providers. The numbers underneath are not the problem: quarterly revenue of about 115.8 billion dollars and profit roughly double a year ago, and the stock fell anyway. The All In hosts, all active investors in the sector, were notably sympathetic to Huang and sceptical of the reporting, which is worth weighing against their own positions.

Source: All In, 20 November 2026, Anthropic Goes Public, Nvidia in Crisis, Markets Tank

The European Commission confirms a cyberattack in which the attackers used an agentic AI tool

Staff data was compromised and payroll and other internal systems were left inoperable for more than ten days. Officials say the attackers used ARTEX, an open source agentic framework that the FBI had previously flagged, and that it had been in use since August. No attacker has been named. The episode pairs it with a JPMorgan disclosure of a third party vendor breach that may have exposed customer names and email addresses, and makes the broader point that agentic tooling has moved from demonstration to live use in attacks on major institutions.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 20 November 2026, Why You Should Try Gemini Neon

Apple is building its own Mac agent while making rival agents harder to switch on

Bloomberg reporting and Apple's own job listings describe a proactive agentic assistant able to write code, browse the web, control apps and navigate the device. At the same time, enabling ChatGPT's computer use support on macOS now takes fourteen steps where it previously took four. Apple frames the added friction as security; critics read it as clearing the runway for its own agent. The timing is the story, and it is the clearest signal yet that the operating system layer is where the next round of agent competition gets fought.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 20 November 2026, Why You Should Try Gemini Neon

AMD drops about eight percent on a short seller report about its China exposure

Hunterbrook Media published research alleging that a large share of AMD revenue traces to China and Hong Kong, a proportion it put at roughly two and a half times Nvidia's, and raised questions about a Chinese manufacturing relationship. AMD denied the claims. The counterpoint aired on All In is worth keeping: firmware found in China does not by itself prove blacklisted dealings, and the China revenue line includes product categories well outside AI. Coming the same week as the new export rule, the report landed on an already nervous market.

Source: All In, 20 November 2026, Anthropic Goes Public, Nvidia in Crisis, Markets Tank

Palo Alto Networks buys Applied Compute for 4.5 billion dollars

The all stock deal was announced alongside a quarterly beat, and it moves a security company into building AI rather than only defending it. The All In hosts read it as evidence that acquisitions get rewarded during periods of high disruption while buybacks signal a lack of ideas. Chamath Palihapitiya used it to support his thesis that a small number of vertically integrated companies will own everything from power generation to the application layer. He is a professional investor discussing listed companies, so treat the framing as a position rather than a forecast.

Source: All In, 20 November 2026, Anthropic Goes Public, Nvidia in Crisis, Markets Tank

Aswath Damodaran walks through how he valued Anthropic, and what he thinks of its share structure

The NYU Stern valuation specialist takes Ed Elson through his Anthropic numbers, his governance objection to the company's multi class share structure, and whether Nvidia is still overvalued now that Huang has gone. His criticism of the structure is blunt and has been made at length in his published work: the arrangement concentrates voting power with the founders in a way he considers self serving. Coming days after the largest IPO on record, it is the most rigorous public attempt so far to put a defensible number on the company.

Source: Prof G Markets, 20 November 2026, Aswath Damodaran On Nvidia's Collapse and Anthropic's IPO

The bitter lesson is coming for software engineering, argues Legora's CTO

Jacob Lauritzen sets out the tension between wanting coding agents to work autonomously and wanting them to follow human designed architecture, and concludes the division of labour is temporary. Whittemore extends the argument: the agent as teammate model is already limiting, and power users instead set objectives, constraints and guardrails and let the system decide its own structure and how many subagents to spawn. A KPMG study with the University of Texas at Austin covering more than 500 early career professionals points the same way, finding that top performers were those who guided, evaluated and refined AI output rather than accepting it.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 22 November 2026, When AI Labs Rival Nations

Grand Theft Auto VI arrives, and the question is whether anything at that scale gets built again

Hard Fork gives the launch a full segment with New York Times reporter Joseph Bernstein, who had early access, and the panel's framing is less about the game than about the moment. The open question they put is whether AI means projects built at this scale and cost will not be attempted again, either because the economics change or because the tooling does. It is a useful counterweight to the week's financial news: an enormous, slow, human made artefact landing in the middle of an argument about automation.

Source: Hard Fork, 20 November 2026, GTA VI Is a Monumental Human Achievement

Everything suddenly feels broken, and the outages are becoming their own story

Both Hard Fork and Pivot spent time on the run of unexplained outages across American infrastructure, following the FAA disruption covered earlier in the week. The connection the hosts reach for is between ageing systems, cuts to national security functions and a tech stack that fewer people understand end to end. Neither show offers a settled explanation, which is part of the point. For anyone running digital services, the practical read is that resilience assumptions built on a stable substrate are worth revisiting.

Source: Hard Fork and Pivot, 20 November 2026
