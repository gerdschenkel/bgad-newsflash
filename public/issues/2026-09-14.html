OpenAI has run out of compute and stopped selling $200 Pro plans

Demand following the GPT-6 Astra release outran available capacity, so OpenAI paused new subscriptions to the $200 Pro tier. Every other plan and the API stay open, and the company framed the pause as the smallest possible step to protect service for existing customers. It is the clearest sign yet that inference capacity, not model quality, is the binding constraint on the frontier labs. Matt Schumer's read: the era of subsidised tokens is ending, and buyers should plan for it.

Altman told staff at an all-hands that he is open to slowing down

Sam Altman said OpenAI could pace the release of new models, most likely in coordination with other labs, while acknowledging he doubts every rival would agree. It appears to be the first time he has personally signalled willingness to slow development rather than defend the current pace. The remark follows chief scientist Jakub Pachocki's call for voluntary slowdowns and Paul Christiano joining OpenAI's board, which together suggest an internal shift rather than a one-off comment.

Anthropic says Chinese labs ran user queries through Claude to build distillation data

A new Anthropic misuse report describes networks of fraudulent accounts used to pull reasoning traces out of Claude for training data, naming Alibaba, DeepSeek and Xiaomi. Anthropic alleges DeepSeek and Moonshot served Claude's answers to their own users, with Moonshot relaying almost 300,000 requests over ten days, mostly to Opus. The company reads this as harvesting realistic production queries for a distillation pipeline rather than faking capability, and notes the relayed traffic exposed sensitive Chinese government and corporate data on several occasions. ChinaTalk's panel described the same pattern as a transfer station that gets PLA-linked subcontractors access to a US model they are barred from buying directly.

Houthi forces used Claude Code to write drone guidance software

The same Anthropic threat report found Yemeni operators using a frontier coding agent to build guidance software for drones. The ChinaTalk panel treated it as the more consequential half of the report: state-actor misuse can at least be addressed through export controls and know-your-customer rules, whereas a non-state group with a credit card and a coding agent sits outside all of that. It is a concrete data point for anyone arguing about where the real proliferation risk from agentic coding tools lies.

A Claude classifier blocked a live virology grant application in May

Anthropic disclosed that one of its classifiers stopped work on a grant application seeking to identify enhancing mutations of the chikungunya virus and select for virulence in live animals. It flagged the request as potentially malicious largely because the research was destined for a military research institute, while acknowledging the same work has innocent vaccine development uses. The case is a useful illustration of how much judgement now sits inside a commercial safety filter, and how little visibility the affected researcher has into it.

OpenAI's maths result has turned into a credit fight over user chat data

The All-In hosts spent a segment on allegations that OpenAI front-ran outside mathematicians working the same Navier-Stokes problem. NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpoge had contacted OpenAI researchers shortly before the 8 September announcement, and OpenAI has conceded it cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their use of its products helped improve the models. For enterprises, the interesting part is not the mathematics but the precedent: a vendor admitting it cannot trace whether a customer's inputs shaped its next release. The hosts all hold active AI and venture exposure, so read their framing of the dispute with that in mind.

Microsoft now plans to triple data centre capacity to 38 gigawatts by 2032

Microsoft is targeting 38 gigawatts globally, up from 12 today, with AI's share of that footprint growing to about a third and CPUs added specifically for agentic workflows. This is the same company that was the most conservative hyperscaler and walked away from leases in early 2025, which makes the reversal the signal rather than the number. The read on the show was that AI infrastructure is nowhere near overbuilt and that elevated capital expenditure has at least five more years to run.

Huang says supply, not demand, is what caps NVIDIA at 70 per cent growth

Speaking at a Goldman Sachs conference, Jensen Huang said demand is well above 70 per cent growth but supply is what lets him confidently commit to that figure. He noted it was the first time in a year he had discussed forward estimates at all. He also put numbers on the unit economics: a single NVLink connected GPU system now costs $8.5 million and contains roughly two million parts, with rack scale orders growing 27 per cent month over month.

The DOJ is investigating whether NVIDIA's $20 billion Groq deal dodged merger review

The Department of Justice is probing whether a non-exclusive licensing arrangement that also delivered Groq's chief executive and chief operating officer to NVIDIA was structured to avoid antitrust scrutiny. Senators had already labelled this class of transaction, including Google and Character, Google and Windsurf, and Meta and Scale, as de facto mergers designed to bypass review. A finding against NVIDIA would reprice the whole acqui-hire playbook the sector has leaned on for two years.

Meta's Muse agent landed better with Wall Street than with users

Muse took 83,000 iOS downloads on launch day, enough for second place in the App Store but modest for Meta, whose Threads launch did 4.3 million. The market reaction was the louder signal: the stock rose 6 per cent and JP Morgan upgraded to buy, on the view that roughly four billion users is a decisive distribution advantage regardless of how good the product is on day one. It is a reminder that in consumer AI, distribution is being priced ahead of capability.

DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash beats its own flagship at a quarter of the cost

The 552 billion parameter model is priced at the very bottom of the market, at 30 cents per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output. Artificial Analysis scores it above DeepSeek's full size Pro model on the intelligence index while costing a quarter as much. The practical use is for teams with basic but highly recurring, compute heavy tasks who are assembling a tiered model stack rather than routing everything to the frontier.

Cognition shipped Devin Voice, and the pitch is that you say it and Devin ships it

Devin Voice pairs OpenAI's GPT Live for the voice layer with Cognition's SWE-2 model on the back end, making interruptible natural speech a first class way to write software. Cognition's Nader Dabit says 90 per cent of his day to day work now happens by voice. The wider argument on the show was that full duplex voice models turn talking into a real interface rather than a novelty, and that the sensible move is to start shifting workflows now, through ChatGPT's native voice or a desktop tool, because the industry is heading there regardless.

ChatGPT for finance ships with paid data feeds built in, and UBS now tests graduates on AI

Updated for GPT-6 and living inside ChatGPT Work, the product bundles connectors and skills for DeLupa, LSEG News and Crunchbase so analysts skip their own integration work, with enterprise security and governance attached. It was built with Morgan Stanley and Evercore and aims at financial models, research and client materials, with OpenAI's Nick Turley saying it is taught to research and support its conclusions like an analyst. The labour signal alongside it is sharper than the product: UBS now requires prospective junior bankers to demonstrate AI proficiency to be hired.

A week into Astra, one team rolled back to the cheaper model after spend doubled

Power users describe GPT-6 Astra as simultaneously the smartest and the dumbest model they have worked with, prone to mood swings and to absurd shortcuts that technically satisfy the request. OpenCode partially reverted to GPT-5.6 Sol after effective spend roughly doubled. The pattern Nathaniel Whittemore draws out is that Astra is well ahead on things nobody was doing before, notably 3D and video work, and close to a regression on the tasks people already had running, which makes it hard to justify for coding today.

Russian commanders are reportedly using AI to fake battlefield progress in their own reports

The ChinaTalk panel discussed an Institute for the Study of War report on Russian commanders generating false evidence of advances to send up the chain of command. The second order effect is the interesting one for anyone who relies on open source intelligence: once synthetic imagery and text are cheap, the OSINT layer Western analysts have depended on since 2022 degrades, and so do the feeds Russian soldiers use to understand their own war. The show notes name ISW as the source without citing a report title or figures, so treat the specifics as unconfirmed.
