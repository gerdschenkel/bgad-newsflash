A leaked Anthropic prospectus puts the first-half net loss at 80 billion dollars

The Wall Street Journal has reported on a draft IPO prospectus carrying Anthropic's first audited half-year accounts. The headline 80 billion dollar net loss is mostly an accounting artefact: about 72 billion dollars of it is a non-cash charge tied to a change in how stock grants are treated, which leaves an operating loss closer to 8.7 billion dollars on first-half revenue of 16.3 billion dollars. The company has separately reported 16.8 billion dollars of third-quarter revenue, up 45 percent on the quarter, but that figure is unaudited and will not appear in the prospectus. The number that should worry a reader more than the loss is the compute commitment: up to 750 billion dollars over the coming years, including roughly 450 billion dollars that cannot be cancelled, due over seven to ten years.

Investors are talking Anthropic down to 1.5 trillion dollars while OpenAI races to raise first

Reuters reports that some investors are pushing for an Anthropic valuation around 1.5 trillion dollars rather than the two trillion that had been circulating. Both numbers come from investors rather than from the company. OpenAI is meanwhile reported to be seeking 30 billion dollars at a 1.4 trillion dollar valuation and trying to close quickly, ahead of the Anthropic listing. The read is that the two largest labs are now competing for the same pool of capital on a calendar, which is a different dynamic from competing on models.

An equity research firm used Claude to design anti-venom proteins and 17 of them bound

Citrini Research, a thematic equity shop with no biology staff, ran roughly 300 sub-agents against a 35-page prompt and generated about 318,000 candidate protein designs. It sent 211 to an independent wet lab, where 17 bound to four scorpion neurotoxins, for a total cost under a couple of hundred thousand dollars. The caveat matters: binding is not neutralisation, and the neutralisation testing is still to come. Scorpion stings cause around 1.2 million injuries and 3,200 deaths a year, mostly children, and a former OpenAI researcher noted the design work took 64 hours while an actual product would take four to seven years.

OpenAI has bought a biotech startup and put its life sciences model on sale

OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio, a four-year-old startup that had raised 20 million dollars building agents to run lab workflows such as manufacturing monitoring and trial tracking. The team moves onto GPT-Rosalind, OpenAI's life sciences model, which has gone from research preview to commercial release alongside a Rosalind Workbench, with Novo Nordisk and Moderna named as partners. The framing is that OpenAI wants to sell tools to pharma rather than become a drug company. Anthropic has gone further in one respect: Reuters reported last month that it runs physical biology lab work of its own, which puts both labs uncomfortably close to competing with their own customers.

Meta's Muse reached 10 million US downloads in 40 days, then leaked 6,800 conversations

Only about a dozen apps have ever grown this fast, and almost all of them were mobile games. Meta is paying for it: Sensor Tower data shows 39 percent of the company's October ad spend went behind Muse, up from 23 percent in September, making it the 21st largest US brand by ad impressions for the month. Retention is the more interesting number, at roughly 30 percent on day seven and 23 percent on day 14 against about 25 percent for ChatGPT, with daily actives at 2.5 million. Researchers at Cleroc then enumerated 6,800 shared conversation links because the URLs were guessable, exposing material including a small business owner's loan application, and Meta pulled the share feature.

A Pentagon official says export controls are the stick if AI labs will not cooperate

Under Secretary Emil Michael said the government would use its full range of executive powers, export controls included, against AI labs that do not work with a new AI task force that met for the first time last week. An unnamed official confirmed controls were being considered as leverage. Michael said he holds no grudge against Anthropic despite its suit against the government, rejected the idea that labs need the state to enforce a slowdown, and said his real worry is Chinese open-source dominance because those firms answer to Beijing. He floated auditing open model weights but said he would rather boost domestic developers, and promoted an FBI vetting service for hiring foreign nationals.

Homeland Security has ordered every federal civilian agency to rip out DeepSeek

A July memo made public this week orders the removal of all DeepSeek models and bans future purchases of DeepSeek products and services. The scope is wider than it first appears: it reaches third-party services built on DeepSeek, including fine-tunes and startups whose products run on those models, and applies even to versions hosted by US hyperscalers on US soil. DHS cites the risk of sensitive data moving to China plus possible malware and vulnerabilities. No published technical work shows risk from US-hosted DeepSeek weights, and the practical effect may be to chill any startup building on open Chinese models while chasing government work.

Enterprise agent adoption is wide and very shallow: 90 percent on coding, 5 percent in production elsewhere

Tenex works with about 100 enterprises, roughly half of them Fortune 2000, and co-founder Alex Lieberman put the numbers plainly. About 90 percent have deployed Claude or Codex, about 60 percent have training and enablement in place, about half have coding agents inside product organisations, and only about 5 percent are running agents in production for anything outside engineering. He also said per-engineer output has roughly doubled over 2026, with the step change arriving when models got better at long-running tasks around December, and that Tenex's token spend now runs at roughly one to two times its employee spend. For anyone planning an AI programme, the gap between the 90 and the 5 is the whole brief.

The compute market now has a price gap wide enough to notice: 40 dollars an hour earned against 6 dollars to buy

Evan Conrad of SF Compute argued that a B300 running inference earns roughly 40 dollars an hour at full utilisation, about 25 dollars at half, while B300 capacity can be bought for about five to six dollars an hour, and that one of those numbers has to change. He said a customer spending 100 dollars on capacity recovers about 25 of it on average by reselling, that cluster financing runs three to five years with blocks paid fully upfront, and that only about 5 percent of total capacity is currently on the order book. SF Compute has moved from brokering GPUs to physical settlement, building enough of a cloud to actually deliver what is traded, and deliberately owns no hardware so it cannot manipulate its own forward curve. Note this is the firm's own market talking its own book.

Source: Latent Space, 20 October 2026, The Programmable Data Center, with Evan Conrad of SF Compute

Ed Zitron argues the chips are selling faster than anyone can plug them in

The episode puts the case that a large share of AI accelerators sold are sitting in warehouses because the data centres meant to house them are not operational, which would mean reported chip revenue is running ahead of actual compute in service. It is the sharpest available counter to the capex-is-justified argument, and it bears on everything from Nvidia's order book to the depreciation schedules underneath the hyperscalers. The specific figures Zitron has published elsewhere, including a claim that roughly three quarters of units sold are idle, were not stated in the show notes, so treat the thesis rather than the percentages as what the episode delivers.

Source: Prof G Markets, 21 October 2026, Is Nvidia Selling More Chips Than Anyone Can Use?

SpaceX posted its first profit as a public company, on revenue up 161 percent

Reported the day before the episode, third-quarter revenue rose to 14.6 billion dollars from 5.6 billion a year earlier, and the company swung to 4.4 billion dollars of net income from a 1.1 billion dollar loss. Starlink and the connectivity business grew 69 percent to 5.2 billion dollars. The stock had been running about 35 percent below its peak going into the print, having listed only in June, so the result lands in the middle of an argument about whether space infrastructure is a growth business or an infrastructure one. Figures are as reported rather than as discussed in detail on the show.

Biohub has 1.8 billion dollars behind a virtual cell, and its head of science says nobody has one yet

Alex Rives, who started the ESM protein language model project at Meta and co-founded EvolutionaryScale, set out the 7 October expansion of Biohub's Virtual Biology Initiative: over 500 million dollars from the Department of Energy across five years, NIH coordination of datasets drawn from more than 500 million dollars of prior federal investment, and 300 million dollars from Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs and Meta together. His own assessment is blunt, that nothing currently marketed as a virtual cell meaningfully simulates one, and that the transformer architecture lacks the multiscale structure cellular complexity requires. His timeline is five years to an information-level model that maps a cell state plus inputs to a distribution over next states, with first drafts of disease atlases inside one to two years. The constraint he keeps returning to is measurement, not compute.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 20 October 2026, A World Model of Life: Biohub's Alex Rives on Virtual Cells, Measurement and the 1.8 Billion Dollar Data Push
