28 November 2026

Terence Tao takes the measure of what AI has done to mathematics

Hard Fork handed its whole episode to Terence Tao, the Fields Medal winner, on how the latest models are reshaping his field. The show notes stay brief, saying only that Tao is watching AI transform mathematics along with the rest of us, and the episode's single further reading link is the New York Times profile that described him as on a war footing. For where he stands publicly, Tao's own blog on 20 November called Levent Alpoge's result an enormous Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis generated with an internal AI model, and he has argued that a result nobody can explain does not yet belong in the literature.

Source: Hard Fork, 27 November 2026, How A.I. Is Transforming Mathematics

Patrick Boyle reads the Anthropic IPO as a warning shot for OpenAI

Ed Elson put the Anthropic listing to Patrick Boyle, the King's College London professor and fund manager, who treats it as a signal about the sector rather than one company's bad fortnight. His argument is that the first big lab to meet public markets sets the terms for whoever lists next, which makes it a read-through risk for OpenAI. He also walked through the forces behind the current market correction, which the show had already called an AI-led sell-off.

Source: Prof G Markets, 27 November 2026, Why Anthropic's IPO Is A Warning For OpenAI, ft. Patrick Boyle

Boyle calls the Huang reinstatement the wrong decision, and the shows now openly disagree

Boyle's position is that bringing Jensen Huang back as Nvidia chief executive was a mistake. That puts Prof G Markets, which titled its 24 November episode around unprecedented corruption, squarely against Pivot and All-In, both of which framed the return as a market-positive restoration. It is the first clean break in the podcast commentary since the resignation. Boyle speaks as an outside fund manager, so treat it as a stated view rather than reporting.

Source: Prof G Markets, 27 November 2026, Why Anthropic's IPO Is A Warning For OpenAI, ft. Patrick Boyle

A SpaceX and Tesla merger has moved from rumour to market question

Boyle took on what a combination of SpaceX and Tesla would mean for markets, handling it as a live scenario rather than a thought experiment. The discussion follows reports this week that both companies have named directors to negotiate terms, which would put it among the largest mergers ever attempted. He closed the episode with his read on the year's lessons and his position heading into 2027.

Source: Prof G Markets, 27 November 2026, Why Anthropic's IPO Is A Warning For OpenAI, ft. Patrick Boyle

Anthropic's record listing is already under water

George Noble, managing partner at Noble Capital Advisors, told Prof G Markets the Anthropic IPO is trading below its offer price, and set out what that implies for the next lab weighing a listing. His caution to listeners was not to over-read daily moves in either direction. The episode title does not hedge: the record-setting debut is already under water.

Source: Prof G Markets, 25 November 2026, Anthropic's IPO Is Already Underwater

A governance scholar picks apart what the Nvidia board actually did

Charles Elson, founding director of the Weinberg Center for Corporate Governance at the University of Delaware, went through the governance failures the Nvidia affair exposed. The question he puts is not whether Huang should run Nvidia, but whether a board that reversed itself inside a fortnight under outside pressure can be said to have exercised independent judgement at all. For any director watching, that is the part that outlasts the news cycle.

Source: Prof G Markets, 25 November 2026, Anthropic's IPO Is Already Underwater

Anthropic spent its first years deciding whether to sell anything at all

Nathaniel Whittemore used the Thanksgiving slot for a history of Anthropic, from a May 2021 founding that billed the company as a research and safety outfit to a business now earning roughly 81 billion dollars a year. The founders initially ruled out selling products, and the alternatives they weighed included licensing models to nonprofits and running an AI hedge fund. They also considered becoming a nonprofit outright, on the view that commercial pressure had bent OpenAI.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 26 November 2026, How Anthropic Went From Anti-Product to $81B a Year

The coding assistant was the bet that made the company

The episode traces the commercial turn to a Dario Amodei memo proposing a lab of about fifty people that would build a GPT-4 level model, then a coding assistant, then eventually AGI. The governance structure the founders chose was a public benefit corporation with a trust meant to eventually select most of the board, which is the arrangement now meeting public shareholders for the first time. The Series A was 124 million dollars in May 2021, at a point when investors openly noted the company had no product and no clear plan to build one.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 26 November 2026, How Anthropic Went From Anti-Product to $81B a Year

OpenAI pulled Clay's ChatGPT apps launch two days out

Jeff Barg, head of AI at Clay, told Latent Space the company was a launch partner for ChatGPT apps and had moved about a quarter of its engineers onto the work for a month. Two days before launch, OpenAI postponed it indefinitely. Clay kept building on the assumption OpenAI would reverse course, and three or four days later it did. It is a plain picture of what it costs to build on somebody else's roadmap.

Source: Latent Space, 26 November 2026, How Clay Builds GTM Agents: Jeff Barg, Head of AI

Half of Clay's agent usage now arrives from outside its own product

Barg says usage splits roughly evenly between Clay's own agent and external harnesses, such as Claude Code pointed at Clay. He treats that as the goal rather than a leak, and says plainly that Clay's agent should not beat Clay plus Claude Code. For any software company with an agent strategy, that is a sharper statement of where the product boundary now sits than most vendors are willing to make.

Source: Latent Space, 26 November 2026, How Clay Builds GTM Agents: Jeff Barg, Head of AI

Do not post-train until you are spending serious money on inference

Clay looked at training its own models for about eighteen months and only recently judged it viable. Barg's rule is not to try to beat frontier models, and not to post-train at all until annual inference spend sits somewhere between 10 million and 100 million dollars, because below that the savings do not pay for the effort. He says Clay expects to ship something in this area soon.

Source: Latent Space, 26 November 2026, How Clay Builds GTM Agents: Jeff Barg, Head of AI

Christiano's warning is about drift, not a dramatic takeover

Nathan Labenz and Prakash Narayanan gave part of their Thanksgiving edition to Paul Christiano's recent comments on superintelligence timelines and takeover risk, read against his 2019 essay on what failure looks like. The scenario they take seriously is gradual loss of control rather than one decisive event. They also press on what would make a safety claim falsifiable, meaning what evidence would actually justify handing a system more control. Note the show notes are generated from the transcript and the page itself asks readers to verify details.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 26 November 2026, AI:AM: Number Must Go Up? Christiano's Warning, Jensen's Return, GiveDirectly's Pitch for the Poor

Tom Davidson argues the intelligence explosion runs into energy, materials and politics

Davidson, a senior research fellow at Forethought, made the case for recursive acceleration of AI research on the same episode and then set the industrial counterarguments beside it. The constraint he takes most seriously is not the supply of ideas but the physical and political cost of building what those ideas require. It is the most useful counterweight this week to the pure compounding story.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 26 November 2026, AI:AM: Number Must Go Up? Christiano's Warning, Jensen's Return, GiveDirectly's Pitch for the Poor

What AI regulation needs next is boring and specific

Mackenzie Arnold of the Institute for Law and AI used the same episode to get concrete: comprehension audits for frontier labs, incident-reporting timelines under California's SB 53, and the unresolved legal questions about agents. The two he singles out are agent identity, meaning who an agent is acting as, and record keeping, meaning what trace of an agent's actions has to survive. Neither is settled in any statute now on the books.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 26 November 2026, AI:AM: Number Must Go Up? Christiano's Warning, Jensen's Return, GiveDirectly's Pitch for the Poor

Rahm Emanuel points at the Anthropic IPO and argues the tax code is broken

POLITICO's tech show, now running as Decoded, put Rahm Emanuel on to set out his economic argument, and the part that matters for this sector is his example: the Anthropic listing and the wealth it created, offered as evidence that the tax code needs rewriting. The point is less about Emanuel than about where AI wealth now sits in mainstream economic policy debate. Worth noting the show has been rebranded from POLITICO Tech and is publishing on weekdays again.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, 25 November 2026, How Rahm Emanuel is handling the AI era
