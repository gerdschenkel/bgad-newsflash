Anthropic ships Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 and takes the top of the intelligence rankings

Anthropic released two new frontier models weeks before its expected listing. Fable 5.2 scored 69.9 per cent on FrontierSWE, four points ahead of GPT-6 Astra, and 49.1 per cent on Frontier Code, while Mythos 5.2 opened a lead of about ten points on cybersecurity. Artificial Analysis put Fable first on its Intelligence Index at 65, five points clear of Gemini 4 Carbon and eight ahead of Astra. The gains cluster in science, knowledge and coding, and are much thinner in agentic knowledge work and terminal coding, where the same benchmark house says it sometimes only matches Opus 5.5.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

The Remote Labor Index nearly doubled, and that is the number to watch

On the Remote Labor Index, a benchmark built from real contractor projects, Fable 5.2 scored 41.3 per cent, close to double Opus 5.5. Commentator Garrison Lovely called it the most alarming figure on the model card. Agentic knowledge work was otherwise mixed, with Fable completing more tasks on one agentic suite but posting a lower Elo. Anyone tracking labour displacement rather than coding skill should be watching this line rather than the coding scores.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

Token prices fell 20 per cent while the cost of finishing a task went up

Anthropic cut per token pricing by a fifth, to 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens. Measured per task, Fable on maximum effort costs 26.54 dollars, roughly 2.6 times Opus 5.5 and more than four times GPT-6 Astra, falling to 9.08 dollars on high effort. Anthropic recommends medium as the default and reserves maximum for finding the ceiling, and one early tester argues low is enough for most work. For buyers who never tune the effort setting, the headline price cut will not show up in the bill.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

A thousand subagents built a bootable operating system in eight days

Anthropic had Fable start from an empty folder and orchestrate more than a thousand subagents across eight days, producing an operating system that boots on a laptop with drivers, file storage, a windowing system, a login screen and an installer. The company also says Claude now writes 90 per cent of its own code changes under human supervision, with Fable held back for stubborn bugs, performance work and jobs such as moving a production service from Python to Rust. The revealing part was the reception: several developers who saw the demonstration simply shrugged.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

Two open physics problems solved, and Fermat's Last Theorem formalised in 30 hours

Anthropic says the model solved two open theoretical physics problems about black holes, largely by grinding through calculations too laborious for people. In biology it designed a method for predicting gene therapy capsid structures and modelled 28 of 34 accurately, and it scored 85.1 per cent on protein design. In mathematics it fully formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours using a thousand agents, where Opus 5.5 had stalled 26 per cent of the way through after 18 hours. These results are getting far less attention than the coding benchmarks that sit beside them.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

A general model beat purpose built robotics models at controlling robot arms

Anthropic demonstrated Fable 5.2 piloting a pair of robotic arms, including on tasks it was never trained for, and outperforming most models built specifically for arm control. On a new simulation benchmark it scored 64.9 per cent, about 25 points above Opus 5.5. The creator of that benchmark asked publicly whether general models are now coming for robotics the way they came for everything else. The hardware and the task set were not specified.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

Security review is where the step change is hardest to argue with

On an independent bug hunt using 105 deliberately planted bugs across two real repositories, most frontier models find roughly 40 to 45. Fable 5.2 found 58, in fewer than half the turns and at lower cost than the previous best result. Separately, one developer reported that the model on medium effort surfaced more than ten severe vulnerabilities that Opus 5.5 had introduced itself, without being asked to look for them.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 10 November 2026, Fable 5.2 is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

A 60 second advertisement in 4K, rendered for 5.75 dollars

A developer built a full 60 second advertisement in a single live streamed session and rendered it in 4K on rented GPUs for 5.75 dollars of compute. Another team used the model to build a working video editor with professional editing, titles, effects, music mixing and a built in agent, and described it as the first model that lets professionals build production grade tools without reading the code. The capability was arguably there before. The price was not.

Anthropic is targeting about 1.4 trillion dollars in the first IPO by a frontier lab

Anthropic is reported to be seeking a valuation near 1.4 trillion dollars when it lists, which would be the first public debut by a frontier AI lab. That pricing will become the clearest read the market has on what frontier AI economics are actually worth, rather than what private rounds say they are worth. It lands in the same week that Jensen Huang's influence in Washington is drawing bipartisan scrutiny, which is unlikely to make the roadshow simpler.

Nvidia has shed about 1.5 trillion dollars in market value

Gil Luria of D A Davidson joined the show to put a figure on the slide: roughly 1.5 trillion dollars of Nvidia market value gone, against the Huang scandal and a jumpy post election tape. Cisco has open sourced four protocols for agents that talk but do not think together

Vijoy Pandey, who runs Outshift at Cisco, described a protocol stack that sits above MCP and Agent2Agent and targets a failure those standards do not address: agents that are connected but not reasoning toward the same goal. Accord gets agents to agree on goals, intent and definitions before work starts, Concord handles renegotiation during execution, Scope is a cognitive observability layer built on OpenTelemetry traces that detects drift from the shared goal, and MAS Lab tests whether the system actually delivered what was specified. The code is on GitHub under Cisco's Internet of Cognition work, building on AGNTCY, which Cisco contributed to the Linux Foundation.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 11 November 2026, Agents That Think Together, Cisco's Vijoy Pandey on Semantic Alignment, Scope, Accord and Concord

Multi agent accuracy does not degrade gently, it falls off a cliff

In a five agent production deployment, one semantic misalignment took task accuracy from 99 to 98 per cent, two took it to 81, and three took it to roughly 16. Pandey also described a travel itinerary system that looked 66 per cent healthy on conventional dashboards while completing none of its tasks, because standard monitoring tracks latency, errors and token spend rather than whether the collective goal was met. Related Outshift work found that in staged debates among five agent teams, 75 per cent ended with every agent agreeing on the wrong answer, and because they abandoned their positions quickly to reach consensus they used fewer tokens, so the cost dashboards logged those failures as efficiency.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 11 November 2026, Agents That Think Together, Cisco's Vijoy Pandey on Semantic Alignment, Scope, Accord and Concord

Coding agents now pick your vendors, and the split is lopsided

In a study of which email API coding agents recommend, Claude Code put Resend first in 64 of 102 extractable recommendations, while SendGrid came first only seven times despite appearing 55 times as an alternative. Resend's chief executive Zeno Rocha argues that documentation, pricing pages and MCP servers are now a distribution channel, and the company publishes a markdown version of its pricing page specifically so agents can read the table. He disclosed 4.3 million users and 120,000 paying customers. A broader and more recent study shows more competition, so the effect is real without being settled.

Source: Latent Space, 10 November 2026, Designing for Agents, Zeno Rocha, Resend

A veteran of the fiber boom argues the AI buildout rhymes with it

Mike Volpi, who shaped Cisco's strategy through the internet buildout, compared current spending on GPUs, data centres and power with the late 1990s fiber overbuild, and argued the infrastructure holds its value even if the market corrects. His summary position is that this cycle could prove more consequential than the internet cycle, which is a striking call from someone who lived through the last one. Weigh it against the source: both hosts are a16z partners, the firm may hold positions in the companies discussed, and a thesis that infrastructure keeps its value sits comfortably with its own exposure.

Source: The a16z Show, 11 November 2026, What the Internet Boom Teaches Us About the AI Buildout, Mike Volpi

Megaspeed is still buying Nvidia chips

ChinaTalk's emergency episode on the Huang affair makes one point that has drawn less attention than the rest: Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud operator suspected of diverting Nvidia chips into China, is still buying them. A Malaysian company affiliated with Megaspeed reportedly purchased at least 4.8 billion dollars of equipment incorporating Nvidia technology between June 2024 and February 2026. The diversion allegations remain unproven, Megaspeed has hired outside counsel and says it will cooperate with US authorities, and Singapore's investigation is open. Most of the episode sits behind a paywall.

Source: ChinaTalk, 10 November 2026, The Spy Who Leased Me
