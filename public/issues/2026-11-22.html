The SEC starts examining how Nvidia accounted for its growth

The US Securities and Exchange Commission is looking at Nvidia's accounting and at whether investors were misled about how earnings were boosted. The inquiry lands while the stock is having its worst run since 2008, having shed roughly 2.6 trillion dollars of value in seven weeks. The same reporting covers Jensen Huang's treatment of staff, doubts inside the board and the company's China chip licences. It is the first time the Huang affair has moved from a governance story to a regulatory one.

Source: The Next Web, 21 November 2026, SEC examines Nvidia's accounting as its stock suffers worst run since 2008

Chamath argues AI consolidates into about ten companies that own everything from dirt to application

Chamath Palihapitiya opens the episode with a thesis that AI will not fragment into thousands of winners but collapse into roughly nine or ten vertically integrated megacorps spanning raw materials, energy, chips, models and the application layer. Jason Calacanis pushes back that agents will let individuals coordinate virtual workforces instead, and David Friedberg argues whole industries get rewritten rather than captured. Worth noting that this is the investment thesis of a man who runs a fund positioned for exactly that outcome, and the episode carries no disclosure of specific holdings.

Source: All-In, 20 November 2026, Anthropic Goes Public, Nvidia in Crisis, Markets Tank, Open Source Crackdown?

The White House weighs a disclosure rule for companies using Chinese AI models

A 22 minute segment, the longest in the episode after Nvidia, covers a proposed federal rule that would require organisations to disclose their use of Chinese AI models. The hosts use it to argue the case for open source AI, on the grounds that a disclosure regime aimed at Chinese weights would chill open model adoption generally. Scope and enforceability of the proposed rule are not settled in the discussion. All four hosts are active investors in the American model layer, which is the side of this trade the rule would favour.

Source: All-In, 20 November 2026, Anthropic Goes Public, Nvidia in Crisis, Markets Tank, Open Source Crackdown?

Prof G Markets blames a culture of no pushback for Nvidia's unravelling

Ed Elson's Saturday read of his Simply Put newsletter argues that the real problem at Nvidia was not Jensen Huang personally but a culture in which almost nobody inside the company challenged him. He generalises the point to Silicon Valley, where founder worship makes internal dissent expensive and removes the check that would have surfaced the problem earlier. It is a short, single argument episode rather than a conversation.

Source: Prof G Markets, 21 November 2026, Jensen Huang's Downfall Reveals Silicon Valley's Biggest Problem

Bob McGrew says the hard takeoff argument does not survive contact with the physical world

OpenAI's former chief research officer, now running the manufacturing startup Arda, argues that automating AI research produces a one time step change rather than a runaway recursive loop. His reasoning is that the binding constraints sit outside the model: compute, GPU supply, wet labs and factories, none of which speed up because the research does. The practical conclusion is that superintelligence arrives at the pace of the physical build out, not the pace of the algorithms.

Source: No Priors, 19 November 2026, Superintelligence Needs the Physical World with CEO and Co-Founder of Arda

Biden's AI advisers put numbers on the American compute lead

Ben Buchanan and Tantum Collins, who ran AI policy from the White House and the National Security Council, estimate the gap with China at roughly 100 to 1 in compute production and 15 to 1 in compute access. Their framing of the export control strategy is that the controls were never the point in themselves, they were a way to open a lead that could then be spent on safety work. They argue the gap could widen further if remote access rental and smuggling loopholes were closed. These are the guests' own estimates, not audited figures.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 21 November 2026, Spend the Lead on Safety: Biden AI Advisors Ben Buchanan and Tantum Collins on the Fate of the World

Anthropic has quietly moved away from threshold based safety commitments

In a segment on what actually regulates a frontier lab, the guests note that Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy has shifted away from the capability thresholds it was originally built around. Their broader prescription is mandatory pre deployment testing, government evaluators embedded inside labs, and pacing mechanisms rather than blunt caps. They judge training run compute caps nearly impossible to set correctly from outside, and prefer threshold triggered pauses to date based ones, which simply create a compute overhang that gets spent all at once.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 21 November 2026, Spend the Lead on Safety: Biden AI Advisors Ben Buchanan and Tantum Collins on the Fate of the World

Data centres have become an electoral problem, and 2028 will be worse

Buchanan and Collins treat the backlash against AI data centres as the binding political constraint on the build out rather than a side issue, pointing to its appearance in the 2026 midterms and predicting it becomes a major 2028 theme. A poll figure of 56 per cent cited in the episode refers specifically to a question about data centres, not AI generally. The comparison case they keep returning to is China's energy build out and the permitting reform that would be needed to match it.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 21 November 2026, Spend the Lead on Safety: Biden AI Advisors Ben Buchanan and Tantum Collins on the Fate of the World

The first serious AI emergency will be cyber or bio, and the bio side is harder to watch

Asked what the first real AI incident looks like, both guests land on cyber or biology, through either misuse or misalignment. They note that the cyber capabilities of Anthropic's Mythos class models were shared with defenders ahead of release. On biosecurity they want AI integration barred from BSL-3 and BSL-4 laboratories, flag that gene synthesis screening was repealed and later restored, and point out that biology models are small, which makes them far harder to monitor than frontier language models.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 21 November 2026, Spend the Lead on Safety: Biden AI Advisors Ben Buchanan and Tantum Collins on the Fate of the World

Bostrom argues for keeping the ability to slow down rather than pausing

Nick Bostrom's position on the a16z show is that the hard problem is not whether to pause but whether the option to slow down still exists when it is needed. He argues against a broad or indefinite pause on the grounds that it tends to harden into permanent stagnation, while warning that alignment gets harder as models become more situationally aware and that some capability gains are riskier than others. The cost of delay side of his argument rests on safely developed superintelligence extending lifespans and addressing disease and poverty. The hosts are a16z partners whose firm has taken a public anti pause position, so the pressure in the conversation runs one way.

Source: The a16z Show, 19 November 2026, How Fast Should We Build Superintelligence? Nick Bostrom

DHH says the agent era runs on Linux, and the reasons are the ones that used to put people off

David Heinemeier Hansson's argument is an inversion: command lines, plain text configuration files and verbose error messages are exactly what repelled mainstream desktop users and exactly what AI agents handle well. He is building Omarchy, an agent first Linux distribution that users configure by asking rather than editing. He extends the point to platform economics, arguing that collapsing software development cost erodes lock in, with the counterpoint from the hosts that when code becomes abundant, taste and craft become the scarce input. Both hosts are a16z general partners at a firm with stated positions favouring open models and infrastructure disruption.

Source: The a16z Show, 20 November 2026, Why the Agent Era Needs a New Operating System, DHH

A professional network where your agent does the networking

Cosign is a professional network built on vouching rather than self written profiles, on the argument that the people doing the most important operating work are the least likely to describe it well. Users can connect their own AI agents to the network so that an agent surfaces relevant people, lists and opportunities before the human starts looking. This is a portfolio episode: one of the two guests is an a16z partner and Cosign is an a16z backed company, so treat the 18 minutes as a product pitch rather than market analysis.

Source: The a16z Show, 21 November 2026, The Professional Network Built for You and Your AI Agent

Tomasello argues language models skipped the part of development that produces agency

The developmental psychologist Michael Tomasello makes the case that agency evolves where environments are unpredictable enough that hard coding behaviour fails, and that it arrives in levels, from simple control loops up to great apes that seek more information before committing. His challenge to large language models is that they started at the endpoint of human communication and skipped the infrastructure underneath it, in particular shared agency: joint goals, joint attention and the ability to invent conventions. His specific claim is that models do not create conventions, where children invent and conventionalise gestures within minutes when the audio drops on a video call.

Source: Machine Learning Street Talk, 19 November 2026, What It Would Actually Take for a Machine to Have Agency, Michael Tomasello

Agents now create more than half of all issues filed in Linear

Linear's chief operating officer Cristina Cordova reports that agents now create a majority of issues in the product, up from roughly 3 per cent a year ago, following its MCP server, an agents platform and integrations with Codex, Cursor and GitHub Copilot. Her warning to other software companies is the interesting part: if agent usage grows while human application usage does not, the product quietly becomes a commodity database sitting behind someone else's interface. Linear's answer was to set aside its planned enterprise feature roadmap and build agent infrastructure instead.

Source: Latent Space, 19 November 2026, Scaling Linear's GTM to $100M ARR, with Cristina Cordova, COO

DeepMind's in-house philosopher says deliberately building conscious AI would be reckless

Henry Shevlin, who joined Google DeepMind in May as its first philosopher, told the Financial Times that capability and consciousness are separate questions and that setting out to build a conscious system at this stage would be reckless. His more practical observation is that people should keep being polite to chatbots, less because the systems warrant it than because of what the habit does to the people. The remarks are the clearest public statement yet of how a frontier lab is thinking about machine consciousness as a policy question rather than a thought experiment.

Source: The Next Web, 21 November 2026, DeepMind's philosopher warns against building conscious AI, FT reports
