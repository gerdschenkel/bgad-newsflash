Jensen Huang has admitted the affair himself, which moves the story past the board inquiry

Nvidia's chief executive sent a company-wide email on Sunday admitting infidelity and apologising to his family, staff, the board and partners. The note did not address the national security questions around Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive that a White House memo identifies as a Ministry of State Security agent. Hard Fork gave the whole episode to the scandal with Stephen Witt, who wrote last year's Huang biography, and the board has now engaged outside counsel while saying it has no reason to believe Nvidia secrets were compromised. China's foreign ministry calls the spy allegations groundless slander.

Source: Hard Fork, 10 November 2026, The Jensen Huang Affair

Rubio reportedly oversaw a classified memo on the entanglement months before it surfaced

The reporting Hard Fork works through says Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of a classified memo in the spring about Huang's relationship, and that Huang was nearly dropped from the president's May trip to Beijing. That matters because it dates official awareness well before the public account. It also reframes the question from what Huang knew to what Washington knew and chose to do with it.

Source: Hard Fork, 10 November 2026, The Jensen Huang Affair

Scott Galloway's case against the Nvidia board is about who pays the directors

Galloway argued the board is too financially entangled to supervise Huang, pointing to one director he said earned roughly 80 million dollars in options over eight years, and he said Huang should go on the precedent of chief executives removed at McDonald's, Nestle and Intel. The board has retained special counsel while stating full confidence in him. Galloway also noted that about 40 per cent of the S and P 500 now sits in seven companies, five of them AI focused, which is why a governance shock at Nvidia reads as a macro event. The director compensation figure is his own and we could not verify it.

Source: Pivot, 10 November 2026, Nvidia CEO's Spy Scandal, JD Vance's 2028 Problem, and America's Brain Drain

Washington cannot name a successor, which is its own kind of risk

Decoded asked tech policy insiders who could credibly run Nvidia if Huang were forced out and found nobody could name a candidate. The episode frames him as a test of whether an individual executive can be too big to fail. Ben Buchanan, the former Biden AI adviser, used the same episode to urge Democrats to take a hard line on AI regulation once they control Congress rather than repeat the last time the party held power.

Source: Decoded by POLITICO, formerly POLITICO Tech, 10 November 2026, Is Jensen Huang too big to fail?

Nvidia now requires Southeast Asian data centres to join its GPU tracking system

Shortly before the scandal broke, Nvidia began requiring data centres in Southeast Asia to enrol in its fleet intelligence system, which tracks where its GPUs physically sit. Set against reporting that Commerce cleared dozens of Chinese firms to handle H200s, some of them little more than empty offices, the move looks like the company building its own enforcement layer rather than waiting for export controls to work. It is also a useful signal of how little confidence anyone has in paperwork alone.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 9 November 2026, Nvidia CEO Admits Affair With Suspected Chinese Spy

Data centre restrictions swept the Colorado ballot and Denver has stopped approvals

Galloway said 41 of 42 Colorado ballot measures restricting data centres passed at the midterms, and Denver now has a moratorium on new ones while a city task force writes recommendations. Mayor Mike Johnston appeared on the episode to discuss it. Swisher added the mechanism behind the backlash, saying developers have been using non disclosure agreements to push projects through without community consultation, naming Utah, Tennessee and Louisiana. The 41 of 42 figure is Galloway's own and we could not verify it independently.

Google has taken over Crusoe's unfinished Wyoming site and will put 17 billion dollars into it

The project was originally pitched at 10 gigawatts and had struggled to find tenants as energy delays and construction costs mounted. Google reportedly pressed Crusoe to exit since the summer and has now bought the site outright. Crusoe still works with Google in Amarillo and is building OpenAI's Abilene campus, so this is a change of landlord rather than a retreat from the buildout.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 9 November 2026, Nvidia CEO Admits Affair With Suspected Chinese Spy

A model found a flaw in the software behind most of the web, and China has patched 4 per cent of it

Chang Che's guest essay for ChinaTalk traces an August 2026 bug found in PHP, which underpins roughly 70 per cent of websites including Wikipedia and WordPress, by researchers using an OpenAI model. Exploitation began before the fix was ready in late October, Amazon detected attempts on its servers, and some governments and companies took sites offline. Censys data cited in the piece has the United States patching over a quarter of 14 million exposed services a week after disclosure, Japan about the same, and China 4 per cent. One security firm called the flaws among the worst ever found in PHP.

Source: ChinaTalk, 8 November 2026, Why the Cyberpocalypse Might Hit China Harder

Exploitation now runs ahead of the patch, and the zero day market is collapsing with it

Google's incident response team reports criminals exploiting newly disclosed vulnerabilities at an average of minus seven days, meaning attempts routinely begin before a patch exists. Nicole Perlroth told the author the grey market for zero days is being decimated and called it the death of NOBUS, asking why a government would pay millions for a vulnerability a model could find for 450 dollars. The piece also sets out an asymmetry in defence: nine of the ten largest United States tech firms run bug bounty programmes against six of the ten largest Chinese ones, and where Apple and Google can pay millions for a critical zero day, Tencent's top payout is about 15,000 dollars.

Source: ChinaTalk, 8 November 2026, Why the Cyberpocalypse Might Hit China Harder

Goodfire argues individual neurons are far more readable than the field assumed

Curt Tigges set out Goodfire's new paper, Neurons as an Interpretable Basis, which claims individual MLP neurons in modern gated models are directly interpretable, undercutting the assumption that helper models such as sparse autoencoders are required. Working on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and its roughly 5 million neurons, the team found that keeping only the top 64 of 512 activations per active expert suppresses about 88 per cent of activations per token while staying close to the original model, with reasoning restored to parity or better after a short fine tune. A Neuron Scientist agent writes and tests hypotheses about each neuron, scoring 74 per cent damage weighted recovery of the KL divergence from ablation, which is not the same as 74 per cent accuracy. Tigges is explicit that attention and the residual stream remain unsolved.

Source: The Cognitive Revolution, 10 November 2026, The Neuron Basis, Goodfire's Curt Tigges on Agentic Interp, Monosemanticity and Reverse Engineering

Three labs are now hinting at a phase transition in the pace of scientific discovery

Google DeepMind vice president Pushmeet Kohli described a genuine phase transition in scientific discovery with releases expected within weeks, Meta's Tom Mann teased unreleased work, and OpenAI's Greg Brockman said frontier models running heavy test time compute are becoming the engine of discovery. Andrew Curran's read is that all three large labs have likely achieved autonomous research with results arriving this month. This is anticipation rather than published result, and worth holding lightly until something lands.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 9 November 2026, Nvidia CEO Admits Affair With Suspected Chinese Spy

Handshake's new life sciences benchmark has most models scoring under 50 per cent

The VIALS benchmark asks models to read gel electrophoresis bands, plasmid maps and histopathology slides, and most score below 50 per cent with some open models under 20. Opus 5 got a gel task wrong and Opus 5.5 picked the wrong slides on a histopathology task. Giving the agents Python tooling such as SciPy and OpenCV helped but kept them under 60 per cent. The team's sharper point is calibration: models express near maximum confidence while wrong, and anything under 90 per cent is unusable in a real lab.

Source: Latent Space, 9 November 2026, What Frontier Labs Buy, A Data Deep Dive with Handshake AI

The market for frontier training data has gone from 1 billion to 10 billion dollars in three years

Garrett Lord sizes it at roughly 1 billion dollars three years ago and about 10 billion now, with a projection, his own, of 100 billion within three years. The stranger detail is that frontier labs often use less than 10 per cent of the data they buy, because training mixes are tuned experimentally, so vendors are effectively paid for optionality. Labs are now also asking for human preference rankings alongside rubrics, to capture the taste that rubrics miss. Handshake runs about 800 internal staff and expects to move from hundreds to thousands of full time domain experts.

Source: Latent Space, 9 November 2026, What Frontier Labs Buy, A Data Deep Dive with Handshake AI

a16z makes the case that the application layer is growing, not being eaten

Partner Joe Schmidt and HubSpot co-founder Dharmesh Shah argue that opportunities in application software are expanding even as frontier labs move into applications, and that founders should avoid replicating the labs' path. Their thesis is that durable value sits in workflows, accumulated customer context and the systems where work actually happens, which argues for starting narrow and solving a whole customer problem. Worth reading as an interested position rather than neutral analysis: a16z deploys capital into the application layer, and Shah runs an application layer company.

Source: The a16z Show, 10 November 2026, HubSpot's Co-Founder on Finding Your Next Startup Idea
