Daily briefing: digital, tech and AI

16 October 2026

Anthropic reports $16.8 billion for the quarter as an IPO nears

Anthropic posted $16.8 billion in third quarter revenue, up roughly 45 percent on the second quarter, with adjusted profitability, positive free cash flow and about 85 percent adjusted gross margins. Those margins may sit before revenue shares with Amazon and other compute partners, which is the number worth watching. Reuters reported the company expects a $110 billion run rate by the end of the year. Reaction was mixed, and the figures landed alongside sightings of an unannounced Fable 5.5.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

CoreWeave’s bonds crack and the AI trade wobbles with them

CoreWeave’s unsecured 2032 bonds fell to 80 cents on the dollar, an implied yield near 15 percent, and the stock dropped 15 percent on a day the Nasdaq fell 1.6 percent. Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson said the equity could go to zero, while chief executive Mike Intrator called the market irrational. Filings show Intrator has sold shares weekly since April. On Prof G Markets, Paul Kedrosky argued the debt load is a leading indicator for the whole AI buildout rather than one company’s problem.

Sources: Prof G Markets, 15 October 2026, CoreWeave Is Imploding And Taking AI Stocks Down With It and The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

ASML says it cannot meet AI demand for at least two years

ASML, the only maker of EUV lithography machines, says it cannot meet AI driven demand for at least two years. Announced expansion plans from TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix and Elon Musk’s Terafab together exceed the roughly 30 percent capacity increase ASML has committed to. That makes one Dutch company the binding constraint on every frontier compute plan currently being announced.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

Epic Games has banned hand written code

Epic has delayed its Marvel and Star Wars shooter to move the studio onto an AI first pipeline, and has stopped engineers writing code by hand. Tim Sweeney framed the goal as more and better output rather than fewer people. Epic’s new launcher, built the same way, reportedly loads faster than Steam but ships with visual glitches.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

A developer rebuilt Photoshop with Claude Opus, and Adobe shares fell

Developer Brandon Thomas produced a clean room rebuild of Photoshop using Claude Opus, then widened the attempt to the rest of the Adobe suite. Adobe stock fell about 3 percent. The counterargument raised on the show is that decades of piracy did limited damage to Adobe, and that companies which built their own CRMs mostly went back to Salesforce. What has changed is that reconstruction now works well enough to be a product question rather than a stunt.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

Bank of America is adding 4,000 people to patch flaws that Anthropic’s Mythos exposed

Bank of America is bringing on 1,000 contractors and 3,000 staff to close vulnerabilities surfaced by Anthropic’s Mythos, adding hundreds of millions of dollars to third quarter costs. Chief executive Brian Moynihan called it real risk rather than a housekeeping exercise. It is one of the first clear cases of a frontier model’s capability landing directly in a bank’s cost line.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

Jensen Huang says Nvidia would cut off an irresponsible AI developer

Huang said Nvidia would, hypothetically, stop supplying a developer behaving irresponsibly, though only after a conversation, and argued that safety is within Nvidia’s control. Nvidia also committed $250 million in compute to UK AI safety research over five years. The remark matters because chip supply is the one layer of the stack that is genuinely concentrated enough for a single company to police.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

Greg Brockman says security spending should vastly outstrip software spending

The OpenAI president argued that cyber and security spend should end up far larger than software spend, and described a defender’s window in which frontier models favour defence before offensive capability spreads. He pointed to a $1 billion commitment to put OpenAI models in front of frontline defenders, alongside partnerships including CrowdStrike. OpenAI also pulled a sizeable group of production engineers off their own projects to red team its infrastructure with its most capable model, an exercise it plans to repeat at every release.

Source: No Priors, 15 October 2026, The Changing Physics of Cybersecurity and Drug Development with OpenAI President Greg Brockman

OpenAI agents have helped settle close to 400 long open maths problems

Brockman said OpenAI agents have contributed to solving nearly 400 mathematics problems that had been open for about a century. The harder questions he raised are how a model communicates what it has done, and how humans verify a result they did not derive. It is the most concrete claim so far that frontier models are producing original mathematics rather than retrieving it.

Source: No Priors, 15 October 2026, The Changing Physics of Cybersecurity and Drug Development with OpenAI President Greg Brockman

Cutting DUV tool exports could leave China with 100 times fewer AI chips

Saif Khan, who worked on export controls at the National Security Council and Commerce across two administrations, has launched a think tank, the Center for Technology and Statecraft. Its first report models the compute balance if deep ultraviolet lithography tool exports to China were halted, and projects China at roughly 100 times fewer AI chips than the United States by the mid 2030s. DUV controls remain looser than EUV controls, which is the gap the report is aimed at. Khan also named robotics as the next arena of US and China competition.

Source: ChinaTalk, 15 October 2026, From Posting to Policy: Saif Khan on DUV and the AI Balance

Brad Smith says blocking green cards could cripple US tech for a generation

Microsoft vice chair and president Brad Smith publicly criticised the plan to block the path to permanent residency for highly skilled immigrants, warning it could cripple the US tech sector for over a generation. The comments follow Vice President JD Vance saying Microsoft can no longer sponsor green cards for its skilled visa employees. A named Big Tech executive attacking White House policy this directly is a shift in posture, not a press line.

Source: The Decoded Podcast, 15 October 2026, Microsoft’s president warns the White House

Payment rails are now issuing millions of agent tokens a week

Terry Angelos said VGS, which secures payment credentials across a large share of e-commerce, is provisioning agentic tokens at a rate of millions per week, built so an agent can pay while chargebacks and identity checks survive. PayPal, Shop Pay and Link are racing to become the default wallet an agent reaches for. Merchants are still refusing agent initiated purchases, liability is unresolved, and agents that shop properly erode the breakage revenue that comes from customers forgetting to cancel. Worth noting that the episode is co-hosted by a16z general partner Alex Rampell and argues the firm’s own agentic commerce thesis.

Source: The a16z Show, 15 October 2026, When AI Agents Start Spending Your Money

Leash Bio’s Minos searches 6.5 million molecules on a budget of 100 experiments

Leash Bio released Minos, an agent sandbox for drug discovery in which language model agents reason about structure and activity in plain language, then search 6.5 million molecules while allowed only 100 evaluations. Its own model, trained solely on proprietary DNA encoded library data, performs close to Boltz on public benchmarks, but turned out to be memorising protein nicknames rather than learning binding sites. The episode also shows models inferring which chemist made a molecule from its structure alone, with that fact predicting activity about as well as the chemistry does, which is a shortcut sitting inside the standard public bioactivity set.

Source: Latent Space, 14 October 2026, Lab-in-the-Loop at Compute Speed, Ian Quigley of Leash Bio

Bumble is in advanced talks to go private at about $1 billion

Ithaca, a new firm led by former Sequoia partner Ravi Gupta and former Meta chief revenue officer John Hegeman, is in advanced talks to take Bumble private at roughly $1 billion. Bumble peaked near $14 billion after its 2021 listing, so the price is about 95 percent below the high. The thesis is that a consumer app with a real user base is now cheaper to buy and rebuild with AI than to compete with.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models

Berkshire backs Bending Spoons’ roll up of legacy internet brands

Bending Spoons buys legacy brands, AOL, Vimeo, Meetup, Evernote and Airtable among them, then runs them with AI and shared staff to strip out cost. Berkshire Hathaway agreed to a $330 million private placement on top of $600 million at the July listing, taking its stake to about 4 percent, and the shares rose 40 percent. It is the clearest sign yet that the cheapest way to buy software revenue is to buy the brand and change the operating model.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 15 October 2026, How the Latest AI Leap is Disrupting Business Models
