Anthropic's own mathematician says he and Claude have proved the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpoge, a mathematician at Anthropic, says he and Claude have proved the 1859 Riemann Hypothesis and its generalized form, released as roughly 17 million lines of Lean. Because the proof is machine checkable, the Lean and Mathlib communities were able to express early confidence in it. The Association for Human Mathematics criticised the result, and some parties say they have already found bugs in the Lean code. The claim landed in the same week as Anthropic's public debut, which has coloured how it is being read.

SpaceX is renting out compute because Musk says it cannot compete in AI

The New York Times reported that Elon Musk told SpaceX employees the company is falling behind in AI, and that renting compute to rivals is more lucrative than building its own models. Musk shared data on Grok's weak performance in a large internal engineering group chat. Gergely Orosz reported that the Cursor team is the only group inside SpaceX that is any good at building AI models. It is a rare admission from a founder who has spent two years insisting the opposite.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 24 November 2026, AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

Z.ai previews GLM-5.4 and moves the reasoning into the weights

Z.ai has previewed GLM-5.4, its first looped transformer model, which pushes reasoning into the weights rather than into long chains of output tokens and so cuts the cost of inference. The company claims 750 billion parameters performing like a model of two to three trillion. It also introduces what it calls generalized coding, where the model produces an artefact such as a music video by writing the code for it. No benchmarks have been published yet, API access follows a safety audit, and open weights come later.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 24 November 2026, AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

Trump says he personally convinced the Nvidia board to take Jensen Huang back

Hard Fork gave a whole episode to how the reversal was engineered, with Huang biographer Stephen Witt and New York Times White House reporter Jonathan Swan. Trump told CNBC he convinced the board to reinstate Huang, and dodged the question of whether he phoned into the meeting. Semafor and CNN reported that he did call in, as part of a weekend pressure campaign run alongside JD Vance, and the White House said he was totally involved. Nvidia's own statement acknowledged input from senior levels of the United States government.

Source: Hard Fork, 24 November 2026, Trump's Mission to Get Jensen Huang Back at Nvidia

Huang will testify in public before the Senate Banking Committee next week

Huang is scheduled to appear before the Senate Banking Committee next Thursday. Democrats including Chris Van Hollen and Hakeem Jeffries have promised investigations, and Republican national security chairs said the board's brief internal inquiry erases nothing. The hearing is now the main venue where the export control questions get asked out loud.

A majority of Americans think the Nvidia affair compromised national security

A YouGov poll found 54 percent of Americans believe the Huang affair compromised United States national security. Newt Gingrich said Congress should treat it as a national security matter rather than a corporate governance one. On MS NOW, Jason Calacanis was challenged by Stephanie Ruhle over his claim that Huang was obviously not guilty, given the president's own exposure to Nvidia stock. The gap between how the story reads inside the industry and outside it is now very wide.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 24 November 2026, AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

Nvidia is facing four parallel investigations, and a Taipei sales manager has been indicted

On ChinaTalk, Chris McGuire of the Council on Foreign Relations and Peter Harrell of Georgetown counted four separate inquiries running at once: the Department of Justice, an internal review led by Wachtell, Congress, and the press. They also noted that an Nvidia sales manager in Taipei has been indicted over smuggling related ties. Their point was that reinstating the chief executive settles none of them, it only sets the clock running on all four.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

The chip bills now in Congress would not have stopped Megaspeed

McGuire walked through the AI Overwatch Act, the MATCH Act and the Remote Access Security Act, and argued that none of them would have prevented licensing to a firm like Megaspeed or addressed smuggling directly. He suggested stronger legislation compelling licence denials may be needed in the next Congress. Both guests said licensing decisions remain the weak point, because a rule is only ever as strong as the licences granted under it.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns

A president picking a chief executive starts to look like state capitalism

McGuire and Harrell said a president effectively choosing the chief executive of a major company is unusual in a market economy and closer to Chinese practice. Harrell argued that government equity stakes can make sense but this kind of intervention does not, contrasting it with the bank bailouts of 2008 and 2009, where executives were pushed out rather than installed. On Pivot, Kara Swisher and Scott Galloway reached the same place from a different direction, calling it the cost of protecting our geniuses.

Source: ChinaTalk, 24 November 2026, Jensen Returns; and Pivot, 24 November 2026, Jensen Huang Returns

A governance scholar says the affair exposed how little a board actually controls

On Prof G Markets, Charles Elson, founding director of the Weinberg Center for Corporate Governance at the University of Delaware, examined what the Nvidia episode revealed about board oversight. Derek Thompson made the related observation that Sam Altman and Jensen Huang are now the two most prominent AI founders to be removed and reinstated inside three years. Taken together the question is whether a board retains any real authority over a founder the market has decided it cannot do without.

Source: Prof G Markets, 25 November 2026, Anthropic's IPO Is Already Underwater

Anthropic's co-founders sold into their own float, and the backlash has started

Filings show that Anthropic's seven co-founders all sold stock in the offering, which critics are reading as an exit liquidity grab rather than a vote of confidence. The stock is now close to the level that would trigger mandated institutional selling. On Prof G Markets, Ed Elson and George Noble worked through what the reception means for OpenAI's own prospects, with Noble's advice being not to trade on day to day swings. Note that Noble runs an advisory firm and is talking his own book.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 24 November 2026, AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

CrowdStrike's chief executive says AI now lets anyone hack at nation state level

George Kurtz told All-In that AI collapses the skill barrier, so low capability actors can run operations that previously needed a state backed group behind them. His longest segment was more mundane: organisations still do not patch, and accumulated technical debt drives both breaches and outages. His line was that hackers do not break in, they log in, with stolen but valid credentials now the dominant route. Kurtz runs a listed security vendor that sells the remedy, so treat the elevated threat framing as vendor sourced.

Source: All-In, 24 November 2026, CrowdStrike CEO: AI Cyberattack Wave, Hyperspeed Hacks, and The Patching Problem

Dwarkesh Patel puts automated AI researchers at 2028 and superintelligence a year later

In an episode working through objections to his recursive self improvement argument, Patel gave a median estimate that the best AI researchers are fully automated around 2028, with superintelligence roughly a year after that, and put about a 25 percent chance on superintelligence arriving next year. He argued AI research is unusually verifiable, with measurable proxies such as pretraining loss and how fast a model masters an unfamiliar game. He also said most recent pretraining gains have come from better data filtering and synthetic data, work that does not need domain experts. He conceded that compute limits the pace and that gains in mathematics may not carry across cleanly.

Source: Dwarkesh Podcast, 24 November 2026, Responding to objections to recursive self-improvement

AI capital spending has quietly become the stimulus programme

Paul Kedrosky argued that the White House is effectively running a private sector stimulus programme through AI capital spending. He pointed to more than a dozen executive orders easing data centre construction, grid connections and emissions rules as the policy scaffolding holding it up. Framed that way, the pressure to keep Nvidia functioning looks less like favouritism and more like protecting the only growth engine currently running.

Source: The AI Daily Brief, 24 November 2026, AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

Nvidia had its biggest day in years and is still well short of its October high

The Nasdaq 100 closed up 6.9 percent and Nvidia up 31.5 percent in a single session after the reinstatement. Even after a move that size, Nvidia remains around 30 percent below its October high and the Nasdaq sits 14.3 percent below last month's peak. The relief rally is real but it has recovered only part of what the past month took out.
