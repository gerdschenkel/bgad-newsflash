A single faulty router grounded every flight in the United States

On 13 November a wireless data router that L3Harris contractors had installed at an FAA radio facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, failed after power up and flooded the network with hundreds of connection requests a second. The resulting packet loss cut radar and voice links to 17 of the 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and the FAA held a nationwide ground stop from 3:54pm until 8:15pm Eastern. FlightAware counted more than 13,000 cancelled flights and over 25,000 delays across the weekend. Officials ruled out a cyberattack and put it down to routine hardware modernisation on the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure, built in the early 2000s and not due for replacement until 2028.

Source: The Verge, 16 November 2026, America's air traffic control system couldn't handle a single bad router

Prof G Markets expects the Nvidia scandal to get bigger, with Lutnick exposed

Scott Galloway and Ed Elson argue the fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation has a long way to run, and that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick could face personal consequences over the Megaspeed affair and the handling of chip export enforcement. Their broader point is the risk of idolising founders, and of a company whose strategy and government relations rested on one person. The $2 trillion in the episode title is the hosts' own framing and is not defined in the show notes. A week earlier the same show put the loss at $1.5 trillion, so treat the number as rhetoric rather than a measured figure.

Source: Prof G Markets, 16 November 2026, Nvidia's $2 Trillion Scandal Is About To Get Much Bigger

Anthropic's listing is being treated as the test case for the whole AI trade

Galloway and Elson call the coming Anthropic IPO a crucial test of the AI trade, with Elson setting out an explicit bull case and bear case before both hosts predict how the offering lands. Reported figures circulating in the same week put the raise at as much as $64.4 billion, with a target valuation near $1.4 trillion, below earlier expectations. It would be the first public listing by a frontier AI lab, which is why a single pricing decision is carrying so much signal.

Source: Prof G Markets, 16 November 2026, Nvidia's $2 Trillion Scandal Is About To Get Much Bigger

The case that Nvidia can thrive without Jensen Huang

In the same episode the hosts argue Nvidia will do fine without its founder, and give explicit year end price predictions for the stock, though the numbers are not in the published notes. That view will be tested against a roadmap, Rubin and Feynman, that chip analysts say leaned heavily on Huang's own judgement calls, and against CFO Colette Kress running the company as interim chief executive. For buyers, the question is no longer the technology but whether allocation and roadmap discipline survive the founder.

Source: Prof G Markets, 16 November 2026, Nvidia's $2 Trillion Scandal Is About To Get Much Bigger

Starship's upper stage was caught at the tower for the first time

SpaceX flew an ambitious 22 hour Flight 15 that ended with the launch tower arms catching a Starship upper stage for the first time, alongside a successful satellite deployment. The planned catch of the Super Heavy booster was called off in flight and the booster was lost. Analysts this week described the ship catch as the clearest evidence yet that the vehicle can be turned around quickly rather than expended, which is the whole economic case for the programme.

Source: Space.com, 14 November 2026, SpaceX launches Starship on ambitious 22 hour Flight 15 test, but aborts booster chopsticks catch

Democrats want to regulate AI and may not be able to move quickly

Two Democratic leaning AI policy conferences, one in Denver and one in Washington, were held just over a week after the party's midterm wins. POLITICO's read is that both showed a real appetite to legislate on AI, built on voter frustration and fear, but also raised doubts about whether new laws can pass quickly even with control of both chambers. The reference point is four years since ChatGPT launched with no comprehensive federal statute to show for it.

Source: POLITICO Tech, 16 November 2026, A tale of two AI conferences

Europe's first high level AI discussion in Dublin read as paralysis

The same episode covers the EU's first high level discussion of AI, held in Dublin on Friday. POLITICO characterises the mood across European capitals as paralysis and defeatism rather than ambition. Set against the legislative energy now building in Washington, the contrast is the episode's actual point, and it matters for any company planning a European compliance posture.

Source: POLITICO Tech, 16 November 2026, A tale of two AI conferences

China adds dredger parts and tunnel boring machines to its export control list

ChinaTalk's reading of MOFCOM's October announcements says Announcement 51 restricts exports of dredger components, including cutter heads, drag heads and slurry pumps, with effect from 18 November, and links the move to China's own island building at Antelope Reef. A separate measure controls tunnel boring machines, with the strictest treatment reserved for countries Beijing designates as sensitive and India the most affected. The pattern is to control the machines that build infrastructure rather than the infrastructure itself.

Source: ChinaTalk, 16 November 2026, MOFCOM's New Weapons of Mass Construction

Beijing controls solar and biotech know how while leaving the products free to ship

Announcement 48 controls 12 photovoltaic technologies and 17 machines while leaving panels, cells and wafers uncontrolled, which keeps Chinese exports flowing and makes it harder to rebuild the industry anywhere else. Five biomedical technologies are split into prohibited and restricted categories, with cell and gene therapy and siRNA platform transfers restricted and a separate rule covering pathogen enhancement know how. Announcement 49 revises drone export rules, auto licensing some short endurance models while extending controls to parts, software and flight control expertise.

Source: ChinaTalk, 16 November 2026, MOFCOM's New Weapons of Mass Construction

Black Forest Labs makes the case for training image models on video and sound

Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell argue that FLUX 3 should learn from stills, video and audio together, because each one supplies something the others cannot. A still shows where the ball is, video shows the back of a head once the camera pans around it, and an impact sound marks the moment of contact when the camera missed it. They stop short of claiming the model understands physics, and the published notes carry no benchmarks or parameter counts, so this is a design argument rather than a published result.

Source: Latent Space, 16 November 2026, Building Visual Intelligence, Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell, Black Forest Labs

The same backbone is doing the video upscaling

Black Forest Labs derives its video upscaler from the FLUX 3 backbone rather than treating resolution as a frame by frame problem. Sharpening each frame independently produces shimmer, because every frame makes a slightly different decision, so temporal consistency matters even when the user only wants more pixels. Podell also flags an unplanned use case that has taken off, people restoring degraded family photographs.

Source: Latent Space, 16 November 2026, Building Visual Intelligence, Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell, Black Forest Labs

AI is moving fast and the economy is not, say Imas and Cowen

Alex Imas, an economist at Google DeepMind, and Tyler Cowen discuss with a16z's Erik Torenberg why rapid AI progress has not yet landed in growth, wages and measured productivity, and what it would actually take to reach double digit growth. They address head on whether frontier lab valuations assume productivity gains far larger than anything visible today. a16z is a venture firm with positions across the AI stack, so treat the framing of that question as an interested one.

Source: The a16z Show, 16 November 2026, Alex Imas and Tyler Cowen, AI Is Moving Fast. Why Isn't the Economy?

The next economic shift may come from agents coordinating, not from smarter agents

The episode's central claim is that the larger economic effect comes from AI agents coordinating at scale rather than from individual models getting cleverer. That moves the bottleneck from model capability to organisational plumbing, contracts, permissions and trust between systems. The same caveat applies, this is argued on a venture firm's own show by one of its general partners.

Source: The a16z Show, 16 November 2026, Alex Imas and Tyler Cowen, AI Is Moving Fast. Why Isn't the Economy?
